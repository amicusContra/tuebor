The Statewide Architecture of Failure

Why It's Not Corruption — It's Design

The word "corruption" implies individual bad actors making deliberate choices to subvert the system. That framing misses what's actually happening.

Michigan's oversight architecture was designed to process complaints in isolation:

  • AGC complaint intake does not cross-reference complainants across cases
  • JTC proceedings are confidential by design
  • SCAO systemic reviews are discretionary
  • LARA entity searches require someone to know what to search for

No single design choice is unreasonable in isolation. Together, they create a system that cannot detect patterns. Not because it chooses not to — because it was not built to.

The Throughput Rates

From the AGC and JTC's own annual reports:

BodyYearComplaintsDismissedRate
AGC20242,3931,62788.8%
JTC202569854097.1%

The AGC dismisses nearly 9 of 10 complaints. The JTC dismisses 97%. These are not statistics about frivolous complaints. They are structural throughput rates.

If you are the subject of a complaint, you have an 88.8% chance (AGC) or 97.1% chance (JTC) of no public consequence. If you are the subject of complaints in three different investigations processed in isolation, your chances are 0.888³ = 70% (AGC) or 0.971³ = 91.5% (JTC). The isolation multiplies protection.

If the investigations were cross-referenced — if the AGC saw that the same attorney appeared in complaints from multiple people across multiple counties — the probability calculation changes. Pattern evidence is qualitatively different from individual complaints.

The system does not cross-reference. That is the design flaw this site exists to address.

Anderson Localization — Multi-Scale

The Detroit investigation validated Anderson localization at N=620,000 (metropolitan scale). The Hemlock subgraph validated it at N=2,000 (small-town scale).

The localization operates at both scales because the same oversight bodies serve both. A complaint from Detroit and a complaint from Hemlock reach the same AGC, the same JTC, the same SCAO. The processing is identical. The isolation is identical. The failure mode is identical.

The physics scales because the lattice structure — the institutional architecture — is statewide. County boundaries change the surface details. They do not change the wave propagation dynamics.

What Breaks the Pattern

1. Cross-Reference Publication

This site exists to do what the oversight system will not: connect the investigations. Every analysis page reads the cases against each other instead of in isolation. The cross-subgraph edges documented above are invisible to the AGC, JTC, and SCAO — because their intake systems cannot see them.

Publishing the connections does what the institutional architecture prevents: pattern recognition across jurisdictions.

2. Reader Investigation

The Investigation Toolkit provides templates and guides for any citizen to file FOIA requests, check LARA registrations, read court dockets, and submit oversight complaints. Every reader who files a FOIA request to Saginaw ISD asking about Ellison entity contracts is testing the system's transparency. Every reader who searches LARA for charter management companies is expanding the network graph.

3. External Probes

Federal agencies (FBI, FTC, DOJ Public Integrity) operate outside the state lattice. Federal courts apply different rules. PACER filings create records the state system cannot seal.

The Anderson localization breaks when the local lattice couples to a larger one. Federal jurisdiction is that coupling.

Every page on this site, every page on detroit.primals.eco, every court filing, every FOIA response, every AGC complaint — these are permanent records. They cannot be unfiled. They cannot be unread. They exist in git repositories with cryptographic content hashes.
