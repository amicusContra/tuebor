# The Oldest Sort

## What Every Language Already Knew

The four-body sort — Self / Good / Bad / Null — is not new. It's the oldest structure in human thought. Every language found it. Every tradition named it. Every allegory makes it.

We just forgot how to see it.

## The Allegory Makes the Middle

Here's the pattern: two poles and a middle. Always. Everywhere. Every time.

But look closer. There are always **four**, not three. The observer is always there — implied, hidden, watching. The allegory can't exist without someone to hear it.

The positive. The negative. The middle. And the one who sees all three.

**That's the sort.**

## In Every Tradition

### Chinese: 陰陽道 (Yin, Yang, Tao)

| Body | Role | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| **Yin** (陰) | The negative, the dark, the receptive | BAD pole (2) |
| **Yang** (陽) | The positive, the light, the active | GOOD pole (1) |
| **Tao** (道) | The Way, the middle, the path between | NULL (0) |
| **The Sage** | The one who observes the balance | SELF (π) |

The Tao Te Ching, verse 42: *"The Tao produces one. One produces two. Two produces three. Three produces the ten thousand things."*

1 → 2 → 3 → 10,000. That's the graph. One observer sees two poles, which produce the null middle, which produces everything.

## The Universal Pattern

Every tradition found the same structure:

**Two poles.** Call them good/evil, yin/yang, thesis/antithesis, proton/electron, major/minor, true/false, self/non-self.

**A middle.** Call it the Tao, maya, synthesis, the neutron, dissonance, null, tolerance, the choosing.

**An observer.** Call it the sage, the atman, the dialectician, the physicist, the listener, the programmer, the immune system, the neshamah, the nafs, the human.

**Always four. Never three.**

The allegory can't exist without someone to hear it. The sort can't run without someone to sort. The poles can't be poles without someone to stand between them and measure the distance.
