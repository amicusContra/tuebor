## Follow the Money

Every 501(c)(3) nonprofit in America must file **IRS Form 990** annually. The 990 is public. It shows revenue, expenses, compensation, related-party transactions, and governance.

Here's how to find and read one.

---

## Step 1: Find the 990

### ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer (Easiest)
- Visit: [projects.propublica.org/nonprofits](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/)
- Search by organization name
- Free. No account needed.
- Shows multiple years of filings

### IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search
- Visit: [apps.irs.gov/app/eos](https://apps.irs.gov/app/eos/)
- Search by EIN or name
- Shows current exempt status

### GuideStar / Candid
- Visit: [candid.org](https://www.candid.org/)
- Free account required for basic access
- More detailed data

---

## Step 2: Read the Key Sections

### Part I: Summary
- **Line 12:** Total revenue
- **Line 18:** Total expenses
- **Line 22:** Net assets

Look for: Is the organization spending what it takes in? Where does the money go?

### Part VII: Compensation
- Lists officers, directors, trustees, and key employees
- Shows **compensation** for each
- Shows **hours worked** per week

Look for: Who gets paid? How much? Do the hours match the pay?

### Part IX: Statement of Functional Expenses
- Breaks down spending by category
- **Line 25:** Total functional expenses
- Categories include: salaries, management fees, legal, accounting, travel

Look for: **Management fees.** This is where Purpose Group's 72.67% would appear. If management fees are a huge percentage of total expenses, money is being extracted.

### Schedule L: Transactions with Interested Persons
- **This is the gold mine.**
- Shows any transaction between the nonprofit and its officers, directors, or related parties
- Includes: loans, grants, business transactions, compensation

Look for: Does the nonprofit pay an LLC owned by a board member or founder? That's a related-party transaction. That's where self-dealing lives.

### Schedule O: Supplemental Information
- Additional explanations
- Conflict of interest policy disclosure
- Governance details

---

## What to Look For (Red Flags)

| Red Flag | What It Means |
|----------|-------------|
| Management fees > 20% of revenue | Excessive extraction |
| Officer compensation > industry norms | Self-dealing |
| Related-party transactions with founder's LLCs | Money flowing to insiders |
| No conflict of interest policy | No governance guardrails |
| Board members who are also paid contractors | Dual roles = dual conflicts |
| "Management company" receiving most of revenue | Pass-through extraction |
