## The Credential Fraud Begins on the Cover

Banks's biography page — the final section of the book — reads:

> **"Honorable Brian R. Banks, M.Ed., J.D."**

Three false credential claims in six words:

1. **"Honorable"** is a title reserved for judges and elected officials. Banks resigned from the legislature in 2017 after his ninth conviction. He is not a sitting official.
2. **"J.D."** implies a licensed legal practitioner. Banks has never been admitted to any bar.
3. The biography states he aspired to *"become a prominent attorney and, then, sit on the bench as an officer of the court"* — admitting the exact goal he has been fraudulently representing as achieved.

The biography further claims:

> "He has earned a Juris Doctor with a Concentration in Criminal Law from Michigan State University College of Law"

True. He earned the degree. He was never licensed to practice.

> "Mr. Banks is a highly sought-after consultant, educator, organizer, assistant, community, political, **legal mind** and now, an author."

"Legal mind." Not "attorney." Not "licensed." The phrasing is a carefully constructed near-claim — close enough that readers assume licensure, vague enough to defend as technically truthful. This is how credential fraud works when the person committing it went to law school.
