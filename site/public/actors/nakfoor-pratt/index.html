The Private Admonishment

In January 2020, Kellie Bartlett filed a complaint against Nakfoor Pratt with the Attorney Grievance Commission. The complaint related to improper trial tactics and included full-page nude photographs of Bartlett and the deputy she had accused of rape.

The AGC investigation resulted in a private admonishment under MRPC 3.4(e) (improper trial tactics). Private admonishments are confidential under Michigan's attorney discipline process and are not published with the attorney's name attached.

The Attorney Discipline Board's 2021 Annual Report records exactly one statewide MRPC 3.4(e) private admonishment for that reporting period.

Source: Clutch Justice, Jul 7, 2026

FOIA Obstruction Pattern

Between 2025 and 2026, Nakfoor Pratt's office produced documented FOIA obstructions in at least three separate cases — all requests for records tied to law enforcement conduct or prosecutorial charging decisions, denied by the office whose conduct is under scrutiny.

Key denials:

  • Jeffrey Snowden's FOIA requests denied as "too broad"
  • Dashboard-camera video denied after Johnson shooting
  • Prosecutorial records denied in Brady-related matters

Source: Clutch Justice, May 26, 2026 · Clutch Justice, Jul 3, 2026

The Johnson Shooting

On October 28, 2024, Barry County deputies shot Jeremiah Johnson, a 37-year-old Army veteran with documented PTSD and traumatic brain injury, five times in the back at his Hope Township home during a domestic call. No body or dashboard camera video exists.

Nakfoor Pratt ruled the shooting justified based on the deputies' account. Johnson's widow, who says she watched it happen, disputes that account. The autopsy documented five gunshot wounds to his back.

The MSP 5th District team that investigated included:

  • D/Lt. Shane Criger — also led the Joey Nagle investigation (Allegan County, similar circumstances)
  • D/Sgt. Bryan Fuller — the same Fuller from the $14.5M wrongful conviction verdict

Source: Clutch Justice, Sep 23, 2026

Outside Counsel Engagement

In May 2023, Barry County retained Cummings, McClorey, Davis & Acho, P.L.C. (CMDA) to respond to two grievances against the Prosecuting Attorney's Office. Attorney Allan C. Vander Laan confirmed the retention in writing.

The Barry County Board of Commissioners authorized payment — then a Board member claimed at the February 24, 2026 meeting they had "no knowledge" of issues with the prosecutor or judicial system. This claim is directly contradicted by the retention letter, payment records, and documented public comment at prior Board meetings.

Source: Clutch Justice, Feb 27, 2026
