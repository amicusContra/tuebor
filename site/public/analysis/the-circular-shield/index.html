The Circular Shield — How the Attorney Grievance Commission Protects the Attorneys Who Protect the Enterprise

October 17, 2026

What Happened

On or about September 27, 2026, a Request for Investigation was filed with the Michigan Attorney Grievance Commission against two attorneys:

  1. Lamar Moreland — Assistant Attorney General. Serves as Vice Chair of the Purpose Charter Academy Board of Directors. Works in the AG’s Criminal Justice Bureau — the bureau that prosecutes state RICO under MCL 750.159i. Governs the school the AG should be investigating.

  2. Todd Perkins — Brian Banks’ personal attorney. Filed the PPO (Case No. 26-114083-PH) to silence the complainant. Has 4 personal bankruptcies and $100K+ in IRS tax liens. His brothers are Judge Sean Perkins (36th District Court) and Judge David Perkins (Probate Court, Chief Judge Pro Tem).

On October 1, 2026, the AGC responded.

The Response — AGC File No. 26-2141

Cynthia C. Bullington, Assistant Deputy Administrator, sent the following:

“We received the enclosed correspondence, in which you list more than one attorney. You must complete a separate form for each attorney describing what actions by that attorney you believe constitute misconduct.”

“Your original correspondence is enclosed. No further action will be taken until you resubmit the Request for Investigation with the requested information.”

They returned the original evidence. They sent blank forms. They created a file number — 26-2141 — and closed it before it opened.

Michael V. Goetz, Grievance Administrator, signed the cover letter enclosing the blank Request for Investigation forms.

Why This Matters: The Circular Shield

The AGC is the entity that disciplines attorneys in Michigan. It operates under the authority of the Michigan Supreme Court (MCR 9.109 et seq.). When an attorney violates the Michigan Rules of Professional Conduct, the AGC investigates, and if warranted, files formal charges with the Attorney Discipline Board.

But the AGC is also a gate. And when the gate chooses not to open, every attorney behind it is shielded.

What the Rules Actually Say

The AGC Can Self-Initiate

From the AGC’s own process document (enclosed in their response to Kevin):

“The Grievance Administrator can initiate a request for investigation in his own name as well.”

Michael V. Goetz doesn’t need a complaint from Kevin Mok. He doesn’t need two separate forms. He doesn’t need anything except the facts — and the facts are public. Moreland is listed on the PCA Board filing at LARA. Moreland works for the AG’s Criminal Justice Bureau. These are verifiable in under 60 seconds.

Goetz chose not to self-initiate. That choice is now documented.

MRPC 1.7 — Conflict of Interest: Current Clients

A lawyer shall not represent a client if the representation involves a concurrent conflict of interest. Moreland’s “client” is the people of Michigan (as AAG). His board seat on PCA creates a direct conflict — the AG’s office has jurisdiction over the school he governs.

MRPC 1.11 — Special Conflicts for Government Officers

A government lawyer shall not participate in a matter in which the lawyer participated personally and substantially while in government service. Moreland literally sits on the board of an entity the AG could investigate under MCL 750.159i.

MRPC 8.4 — Misconduct

It is professional misconduct for a lawyer to engage in conduct that is prejudicial to the administration of justice. An AAG governing an enterprise that the AG should be investigating is definitionally prejudicial to the administration of justice.

MRPC 3.1 — Meritorious Claims

A lawyer shall not bring or defend a proceeding, or assert or controvert an issue therein, unless there is a basis in law and fact for doing so. Perkins filed a PPO against a parent for filing FOIA requests and regulatory complaints — both protected activities under the First Amendment and UPEPA (Act 52 of 2025).
