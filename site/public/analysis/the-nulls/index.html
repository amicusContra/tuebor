The Nulls — Anchor Rock, CitizenDetroit, SCBC, and the Organizations Nobody Can Classify

October 14, 2026

The Nulls

The membrane classifies entities into four categories: self (does what it claims), partial (exists in both worlds), non-self (extractive), and null (can't tell).

Null is the fog. Here are some of the things hiding in it.

Anchor Rock Foundation

Edges: 6. Sort: null. Tier: 5.

Anchor Rock Foundation and its related entity Anchor Rock are both null-classified.

What does Anchor Rock do? The graph can't tell. 6 edges connect it to the Banks ecosystem, but the edges don't reveal enough about its function for the 36 classification methods to assign a sort.

Anchor Rock could be a legitimate community foundation. It could be another extraction vehicle. The membrane doesn't know because nobody has looked.

CitizenDetroit Action Group

Edges: 6. Sort: null. Tier: 5.

CitizenDetroit connects to Banks and to PCA board members. It appears in the governance layer of the investigation — people who participate in "civic engagement" around charter schools.

The name sounds democratic. Citizen. Detroit. Action. Group.

But the classification is null. The membrane can't determine whether this is genuine civic engagement or astroturf — a manufactured grassroots organization designed to create the appearance of community support.

In the charter school ecosystem, astroturf organizations are common. They carry names that evoke community and democracy. They're funded by the same foundations that fund the schools. They advocate for the same policies.

Is CitizenDetroit astroturf? Null. The membrane doesn't know. That's the whole point of null.

School Choice Is The Black Choice Tour (SCBC)

Edges: 11. Sort: null. Tier: 4.

SCBC is governed by Roland Martin — the journalist funded by Walton. It has 11 edges, more than many classified entities.

The tour promotes school choice in Black communities. It frames charter schools as a civil rights issue — choosing where your child goes to school is choosing freedom.

But the tour is governed by a journalist funded by the same foundation that funds the charter schools the tour promotes. And the charter schools include PCA, where 3% of Black children can do math.

11 edges. Null. The membrane sees an entity that sounds like civil rights and looks like marketing. It can't tell which is real because both are.

NuVision Foundation

Edges: 2. Sort: null.

Also governed by Roland Martin. Also null.

NuVision Foundation and NuVision Inc form a paired nonprofit/for-profit structure. Martin governs both.

Two edges isn't enough for the membrane to classify. NuVision sits in the fog — connected to Martin, who is connected to Walton, who is connected to PCA, who is connected to Banks. But the direct lines are thin.

What Null Means

Null is not a verdict. Null is a gap.

When the membrane classifies something as null, it means:

  1. The entity exists in the investigation graph
  2. It has edges connecting it to other entities
  3. The 36 classification methods don't have enough signal to assign self, partial, or non-self
  4. The entity is unexamined, not exonerated

Every null entity is an invitation to look closer.

Of the 341 classified entities in the investigation:

  • 73 are null
  • Most of those 73 have fewer than 5 edges
  • They cluster around Banks like satellites in low orbit — close enough to feel gravity, too small to resolve

The Fog Strategy

If you wanted to hide extraction, you would:

  1. Create entities with vague names (Foundation, Action Group, Tour)
  2. Give them minimal public filings
  3. Connect them to your network through governance edges
  4. Keep them small enough that classifiers can't reach them
  5. Use them for specific functions (events, advocacy, media) that generate legitimacy

Every one of those steps produces a null entity. Not because the entity is necessarily hiding something. But because the structure is indistinguishable from hiding something.

The membrane can't tell the difference. That's the strategy.
