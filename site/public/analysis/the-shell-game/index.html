How the Game Works

The shell game has three moves:

Move 1: Extract. Purpose Group LLC takes 72.67% of PCA's revenue. This is the primary extraction.

Move 2: Disperse. The money moves from Purpose Group into other LLCs. Each LLC has a different "purpose" — consulting, property, promotion, catering, upfitting, power washing.

Move 3: Obscure. By spreading across 16 entities, no single filing shows the full picture. You'd have to pull LARA records for all 16 to see the constellation.

The graph sees all 16 because it doesn't look at one filing at a time. It looks at 131,329 edges at once.

Every road leads to the same pocket.

Why Null Matters

14 of 16 LLCs are classified as null.

Null doesn't mean "not guilty." Null means the membrane can't tell.

When an entity has 2-3 edges and no clear money flow or governance pattern, the 36 classification methods can't assign a sort class. The entity exists in a fog.

But null entities clustered around a non-self entity are the fog. They're the smoke that obscures the fire.

If you see 14 unclassifiable LLCs all owned by a man with 9 convictions who runs a school with 3% math proficiency — the nulls aren't innocent. They're unexamined.

That's a gap. And gaps are where the game is played.

The LARA Trail

Every LLC in Michigan must be registered with LARA (Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs).

Every one of these 16 LLCs has a LARA filing. Every filing has a registered agent. Every registered agent is Brian Banks or an entity connected to Brian Banks.

The filings are public. You can pull them right now.

Nobody did. Until now.
