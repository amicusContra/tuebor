## III. THE $1,000,000 MONEY LAUNDERING COMPLAINT

**Meyers v. RFFW LLC d/b/a RFFW Ballot Question Committee AND Renae Moore, Treasurer**

Filed September 7, 2023, with the Michigan Department of State, Bureau of Elections.

### The Facts

RFFW LLC was a Michigan limited liability company that:
1. **Failed to register** as a ballot question committee for **351 days** (violation of MCL 169.224(1))
2. Transferred **at least $1,000,000** to Reproductive Freedom For All without disclosure
3. Only registered after a complaint was filed AND the Department **rejected RFFW's claims** that it wasn't a ballot question committee
4. Filed a **Dissolution Statement** while still having significant unreported expenditures
5. Reported **zero expenditures** despite Dykema's vigorous legal defense of RFFW (which was never reported as a legal expenditure)
6. Paid a **$1,300 fine** — which was also never reported

### Moore's Role

**Renae Moore was the named Treasurer** of RFFW LLC. The complaint calls out that Moore's address is the Dykema law firm in Lansing.

The complaint explicitly accuses Moore of **"Filing Fraudulent Campaign Finance Reports"** — the headline of the complaint reads:

> *"Campaign Finance Complaint against RFFW LLC d/b/a RFFW Ballot Question Committee and Renae Moore, Treasurer for Filing Fraudulent Campaign Finance Reports"*

### The Pattern

The complainant noted this was **not the first time** this structure was used. In a 2014 matter involving Home Care First, Inc., the same technique — forming a nonprofit instead of a ballot question committee — was used to **"thwart the disclosure purposes of the Michigan Campaign Finance Act and deprive the electorate of any meaningful opportunity to discover the source of funds prior to Election Day."**

The RFFW complaint was resolved on September 4, 2023 — with **no further action indicated.**

Source: [michigan.gov/sos — Meyers v. RFFW](https://www.michigan.gov/sos/-/media/Project/Websites/sos/CFR-Complaints/2023/Meyers-v-RFFW-2.pdf)
