The Structural Argument

This case is the first major test of Michigan's Uniform Public Expression Protection Act (UPEPA).

The defendant — a journalist — was sued for published reporting about the plaintiff's documented conduct in public court proceedings. The plaintiff's own client has been convicted of crimes arising from the same online platform the plaintiff joined. The plaintiff then invoked the same anti-SLAPP statute in a separate case to protect his own litigation from a counterclaim.

If the UPEPA motion succeeds: the SLAPP suit is dismissed, fees shift to the plaintiff, and the statute works as designed.

If it fails: the first thing Michigan's anti-SLAPP statute did was fail to protect a journalist from a SLAPP suit by an attorney who simultaneously uses the statute offensively.
