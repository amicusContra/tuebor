## The Nulls

The membrane classifies entities into four categories: **self** (does what it claims), **partial** (exists in both worlds), **non-self** (extractive), and **null** (can't tell).

Null is the fog. Here are some of the things hiding in it.

## What Null Means

Null is not a verdict. Null is a **gap.**

When the membrane classifies something as null, it means:
1. The entity exists in the investigation graph
2. It has edges connecting it to other entities
3. The 36 classification methods don't have enough signal to assign self, partial, or non-self
4. The entity is **unexamined**, not exonerated

Every null entity is an invitation to look closer.

Of the 341 classified entities in the investigation:
- 73 are null
- Most of those 73 have fewer than 5 edges
- They cluster around Banks like satellites in low orbit — close enough to feel gravity, too small to resolve

## The Fog Strategy

If you wanted to hide extraction, you would:
1. Create entities with vague names (Foundation, Action Group, Tour)
2. Give them minimal public filings
3. Connect them to your network through governance edges
4. Keep them small enough that classifiers can't reach them
5. Use them for specific functions (events, advocacy, media) that generate legitimacy

Every one of those steps produces a null entity. Not because the entity is necessarily hiding something. But because the structure is indistinguishable from hiding something.

The membrane can't tell the difference. That's the strategy.
