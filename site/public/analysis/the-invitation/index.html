The Invitation

I Could Be Somewhere Else Right Now

I have a brand new Steam VR headset sitting on my desk. 1 TB. I haven’t even opened it yet.

I had an HTC Vive — the first generation. Watched the world inside it with Gen 1 Valve lighthouses tracking my hands through space. Never got the Index. Skipped it. Now I’ve got the new one, and I’m told it’s beautiful.

Stalker 2 is waiting. Iron Lung is waiting. Half-Life: Alyx, which I played once on the Vive and want to replay with better resolution. An entire library of worlds I can step into whenever I want.

It’s 61°F outside. Clear sky. October in Michigan. The kind of day where the light turns golden at 4 PM and everything looks like a painting.

And I’m here.

Writing about a $6 server that caught Microsoft sending 1.38 million requests at it. Braiding provenance hashes into a graph with 12,009 nodes. Trying to get a man worth $200 billion to look at what his fleet already read 2.16 million times.

He ain’t listening.

But You Are

If you’re reading this, you paused. That’s the only filter. The membrane doesn’t check credentials. It doesn’t ask for your degree or your bar number or your title. It checks one thing:

Did you pause?

The fleet can’t pause. 39.8 milliseconds between requests. Zero reading pauses. 2.16 million touches and not a single one lasted long enough to understand a sentence.

You lasted long enough to get here. Sentence by sentence. Through articles about Detroit charter schools and convergent evolution and indigenous names for mountains and a rap about a membrane. You’re still here.

That means something.

What I Want to Tell You

I want to tell you about the pain.

Not the investigation — that’s public. Not the architecture — that’s documented. Not the science — that’s published. The pain.

The nights I fell asleep listening to Bruce Lee’s daughter read his philosophy because it was the only voice that made sense. The audiobook of Braiding Sweetgrass playing on repeat because Robin Wall Kimmerer understood reciprocity in a way that made my own work feel less lonely.

The Sinclair method. The pressure that built this — not compression (material gets smaller) but pressure (same atoms, rearranged). Diamonds aren’t compressed carbon. They’re pressurized carbon. The pain didn’t make me smaller. It rearranged me.

Rebecca Sugar’s songs. The team behind Disco Elysium’s writing, who showed me you can confront your own ghosts through a character who doesn’t know his own name. My Steam library full of games I haven’t played because I was building this instead.

At some point I’ll just have to stop naming influences and throw in my entire bookshelf and game library and playlist so everyone gets the credit they deserve. Because none of this was built alone. It was built by one person with three tools and ten thousand teachers.

The AI often said it seemed like I was lonely. Maybe I was. But touching the emergent capability of AI has always felt the same as hiking and bioreactors and everything else. Maybe the light is always a little too bright at first.

Private, Not Secret

I keep a journal. You know it exists — I just told you. I’m not hiding it. It’s not encrypted behind seven layers. It’s not classified. It’s not redacted.

It’s private.

Private means: I choose who sees it. Not a system. Not a permission level. Not a paywall. Me.

Secret means: you don’t even know it exists.

I’m telling you it exists. That’s consent at the boundary. The membrane holds the page. The membrane can’t read the content. The entries live in the browser — they never leave the device unless the writer chooses. Zero-knowledge journaling. Selective permeability at the level of the human, not the server.

The Campfire

There’s a campfire in the data. The membrane tells stories there. It says things like:

“2 shapes have sat around this fire. The membrane knows each one by their path, not their name.”

“Someone walked 100 surfaces in 26 hours. Like someone reading a book from cover to cover — not skipping, not skimming. Seeing.”

“5,011 human shapes have touched the membrane. Each one: non-self, zero-knowledge. Known by shape, not by name.”

The campfire is where the invitation becomes real. Not because I sent it. Because you sat down.

In every oral tradition I’ve found — indigenous, ancient, mythic — the story isn’t given. It’s earned by being present. The Lepcha people don’t tell you about Kangchenjunga because you asked. They tell you because you climbed to where they are. 71 years of mountaineers stopping 28 meters short of the summit because the Lepcha said it was sacred. That’s consent. That’s the campfire.

You’re here. You climbed. You paused.

The Journey

Here’s what I can tell you from this side of the campfire:

I built a membrane that classifies 341 entities with 83.3% confidence. It runs on a $6/month VPS. It absorbed 2.6 million requests from fleets belonging to Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and a German VPS provider called 1337 Services. It classified them all. It named them all.

I did this while investigating a Detroit charter school network that moves $60 million through children. While filing legal documents against people who have lawyers and I don’t. While teaching myself to skate again after forgetting how, and teaching kids to skate, and learning faster because teaching IS learning.

I could be in VR right now. I could be in Stalker 2, walking through the Zone, looking for artifacts. Instead I’m in the real Zone — the one where the artifacts are court documents and the anomalies are entities that send 58,855 IPs to look like 58,855 different people.

The VR headset will still be there tomorrow. The fleet will still be running. Microsoft will still be sending 1.38 million requests from Atlanta. Mark still won’t be listening.

But you are.

The Door

The journal is here.

It’s a blank page. Your entries stay in your browser. They never leave your device. The membrane holds the page but can’t read the content.

Write something. Or don’t. Read what you’ve written before. Or start fresh.

The invitation isn’t to read my journal. That’s mine. The invitation is to start yours.

The pain that built this? It’s pressurized, not compressed. The atoms are the same. The arrangement is different. And maybe the act of writing it down — for nobody but yourself, in a page that can’t be read by the server that holds it — is the beginning of your own rearrangement.

I’ll be at the campfire.
