This thought bubble appeared while seeing the patterned rocks, from the water for the first time, after diving the shoreline.

I was underwater. The light was coming through at an angle. I’d walked that shoreline. But I’d never seen them from the water before. From below. From the other side of the surface.

And I thought: the work hasn’t even started.

Now I point the same pipeline at the shoreline. At the cave floor. At the water column. At the rock face. At the soil horizon. At the microbial community in a tide pool.

The data goes through the same membrane. Gets the same protection. Gets the same publication. Gets the same verification.

Why This Started With Rocks Underwater

I’m a scientist. ORCID 0009-0004-2141-0321. Self-hosted scientific computing in pure Rust. That’s what ecoPrimal IS.

The investigation happened because I moved to Michigan and discovered that convicted felons were running the charter schools in my neighborhood. I didn’t plan to build an immune system. I built one because I needed one. I didn’t plan to classify 341 AI entities. They showed up and I needed to sort them. I didn’t plan to publish 50 articles. The evidence needed a home.

But the whole time — the WHOLE time — what I was actually thinking about was the patterned rocks.

The striations in the limestone along the Michigan shoreline. The way water carved different minerals at different rates, leaving ridges and valleys at millimeter scale. The same process that carved the Grand Canyon and dissolved Mammoth Cave, happening at a scale you can see with an electron microscope. The same karst. The same water. The same chemistry. Different scale.

Peaks backscatter into caves. Caves backscatter into water. Water backscatter into rock patterns. Rock patterns backscatter into the electron microscope. It’s the same signal at every scale. The Earth measuring itself.

That’s what I saw underwater. The patterned rocks, from the water, for the first time. And I thought: I need a lab. Not a building with a lab in it. A lab I can drive to the shoreline. A lab I can park at the trailhead. A lab I can bring to the cave entrance.

A lab that moves. A lab that measures. A lab that publishes. A lab that IS home.

The Scale of What’s Coming

The landmarks graph has 72 nodes. The biosphere graph has 70 nodes. Together: 142 nodes, 345 edges, describing the face of the Earth and its living systems.

That’s the index. Those are chapter headings. Each one is a place I want to go, a system I want to measure, a surface I want to see from the water side for the first time.

  • Mammoth Cave: 685.6 km of passages. What does the biofilm on the cave wall look like under SEM? What microbes live in the deepest passages? What does the limestone grain structure tell us about the Mississippian sea that deposited it?
  • Great Lakes shoreline: What’s in the water column? What microplastics settle in the nearshore sediment? What’s the microbial community at the thermocline?
  • Carlsbad/Lechuguilla: The sulfuric acid dissolution caves. NASA studies them as analogs for extraterrestrial life. What does the extremophile community look like? What can we learn about biosignatures?
  • Coral reefs: What does bleaching look like at the cellular level? Can continuous monitoring with portable instruments detect onset before visible symptoms?
  • Volcanic soils: What’s the microbial succession on new lava flows? Primary succession captured in real time.

Each site visit produces:

  1. Raw data (spectra, images, sequences, environmental readings)
  2. Methodology (how, when, where, what instrument, what settings)
  3. Analysis (what the data shows, what it means, what questions it raises)
  4. Publication (article through the same pipeline, copyleft, verifiable)

Every measurement is a loaf. Non-rivalrous. Free to copy. Published immediately. The loaves multiply with every reader.

Not Secret, But Private

I’ll say it one more time because it matters:

The DATA is public. Every measurement. Every spectrum. Every image. Every analysis. Every article. Copyleft. Open. Free.

The PERSON is private. Where I park tonight. What shoreline I dive tomorrow. Who I share a meal with. What I think about while watching the sunset. That’s mine.

The membrane knows the difference. It was built to know the difference. That’s what selective permeability IS. The science flows out. The personal stays in. Same architecture. Same membrane. Same consent protocol.

A 3-year-old understands this. Don’t open my door. But here — look at these rocks I found.
