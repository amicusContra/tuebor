## What This Page Is

This is not about one school. This is about a system.

Brian Banks runs two charter schools in Detroit — MacDowell Prep and Purpose Charter
Academy. Together they serve about 362 children. But Banks operates within a **$288M+/year
charter school system** involving 62+ schools, 10 authorizers, and a regulatory framework
that Michigan's own [Citizens Research Council](https://crcmich.org/wp-content/uploads/rpt409_Charter_School_Oversight-2020-1.pdf)
has called structurally inadequate.

To understand how a 9-time convicted felon runs schools for children, you have to
understand the system that makes it possible.

## 1. Who Runs Education in Detroit

Detroit's children attend school in three parallel systems. None of them answer to
the same authority.

| System | Enrollment | Governance |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| **DPSCD** (traditional public) | ~48,000 | Elected school board, superintendent |
| **Charter schools** | ~30,000 | 10 different authorizers, mostly universities |
| **Out-of-district** | ~27% of residents | Suburban public and charter schools |

More than half of Detroit's school-age children attend schools outside the traditional
public district. The charter sector alone handles **$288M+/year** in state per-pupil
funding — public money flowing through privately governed institutions with widely
varying oversight.

### The 10 Authorizers

Michigan allows universities, community colleges, intermediate school districts, and
local school districts to authorize charter schools. Detroit's 62+ charters are
authorized by **10 different entities**:

| Authorizer | Type | Detroit Schools | Location | Notes |
|------------|------|-----------------|----------|-------|
| **GVSU** (Grand Valley State) | University | ~25-35 | Grand Rapids | Largest Detroit authorizer. 78 schools statewide. |
| **CMU** (Central Michigan) | University | ~9 | Mt. Pleasant | Authorized MacDowell Prep (Banks). |
| **Oakland University** | University | 5-7 | Rochester | [2025 Authorizer Report](https://www.oakland.edu/media/Oakland/Assets/psa/files/OU-Authorizer-Report-2025_Final_ADA.pdf) |
| **EMU** (Eastern Michigan) | University | Several | Ypsilanti | Authorized DPSA (Bettison on board). |
| **DPSCD** | District | 7 | Detroit | Authorized PCA (Banks, 2025). |
| **Ferris State** | University | Several | Big Rapids | |
| **Saginaw Valley State** | University | Several | University Center | |
| **Northern Michigan** | University | Few | Marquette | |
| **Bay Mills CC** | Comm. College | Few | Brimley | |
| **Wayne RESA** | ISD | Few | Wayne County | |

**The geography tells the story:** Most Detroit charter schools are authorized by
institutions located **50-250 miles away**. Grand Valley State University, the largest
authorizer of Detroit charters, is in Grand Rapids — a 2.5-hour drive. Central
Michigan University, which authorized MacDowell, is in Mt. Pleasant — 2.5 hours north.

These institutions collect a **3% fee** on each school's state aid allocation
(~$288/pupil/year). For a university authorizing 30 Detroit schools, that's roughly
**$2.6M/year in authorization revenue** — from children they never see.

Source: [Detroit PEER — K-12 Education System Overview (2025)](https://detroitpeer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Detroit-K-12-Education-System-Overview.pdf), [MCCSA Annual Report 2023-24](https://www.lssu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/MCCSA_Annual_Report_2023-2024_FINAL.pdf)

## 2. The Major Operators — Who Actually Runs the Schools

Michigan leads the nation in for-profit charter school management. **61% of Michigan
charter schools use for-profit management companies** — the highest rate of any state.

### Large Chains

| Operator | Schools in Detroit | Type | Scale | Notes |
|----------|-------------------|------|-------|-------|
| **[National Heritage Academies](https://www.nhaschools.com)** | 16 | For-profit CMO | 103 schools, 9 states | Grand Rapids HQ. $1B+ revenue est. |
| **[University Prep (U Prep)](https://www.uprepschools.com)** | 10 | Nonprofit | 3 districts | Detroit-based. GVSU-authorized. |
| **[Cornerstone Education Group](https://cornerstoneschools.org)** | ~6 | Nonprofit | Detroit only | Founded by Clark Durant. |
| **Covenant Schools** | 4 | Network | Detroit campuses | Central, East, Southwest, Spectrum. |
| **Choice Schools Associates** | Several | For-profit EMO | Michigan | Manages Dove Academy and others. |

### Single-School Operators (Like Banks)

Self-managed single-school operators receive the **least oversight** of any category.
With no parent organization, no network reporting, and an authorizer hundreds of
miles away, these schools operate with near-total autonomy.

Banks is a self-managed operator. He is superintendent of both MacDowell and PCA.
Purpose Group LLC (his private company) takes 72.67% of MacDowell's revenue as a
"management fee." There is no external management company to provide a check —
**he is the management company**.

Source: [Detroit Free Press charter investigation (2014)](https://www.freep.com/story/news/local/michigan/2014/06/22/michigan-spends-1b-on-charter-schools-but-fails-to-hold/77155074/)

## 3. Performance — Nobody's Winning

Neither charter schools nor DPSCD are delivering acceptable outcomes for Detroit's
children. Both sectors perform catastrophically below state averages.

### M-STEP 2023-24 Results

| Subject | Detroit Charters | DPSCD | Statewide | Gap |
|---------|-----------------|-------|-----------|-----|
| **Math** | **9.7%** | **10.5%** | **35.1%** | Charters: -25.4 pp below state |
| **Reading** | **20.6%** | **16.0%** | **40.5%** | Charters: -19.9 pp below state |
| **Science** | 15.4% | — | — | — |
| **Social Studies** | 4.3% | — | — | — |

**School Index (composite):** Detroit charter average = 45.8, Detroit non-charter = 45.4.
Essentially a statistical tie.

### Where MacDowell Fits

MacDowell's **3% math proficiency** is not just below the state average — it is below
the already-catastrophic Detroit charter average (9.7%). MacDowell ranks **1,443 out of
1,488** Michigan schools — bottom 3%.

| Metric | MacDowell | Detroit Charter Avg | State Avg |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|-----------|
| Math proficiency | **3%** | 9.7% | 35.1% |
| Reading proficiency | ~12% | 20.6% | 40.5% |
| Revenue extraction to LLC | **72.67%** | Varies | ~0% |
| Credentialed staff | 14% (2/14) | ~85%+ | ~95%+ |

The question is not whether charters outperform DPSCD — the data shows they do not.
The question is **why both sectors are failing at the same rate** while the charter
sector extracts $288M+/year through privately controlled LLCs with limited
disclosure requirements.

Source: [Chalkbeat — Detroit charter M-STEP (2024)](https://www.chalkbeat.org/detroit/2024/10/07/detroit-charter-students-m-step-results/), [Chalkbeat — DPSCD M-STEP (2024)](https://www.chalkbeat.org/detroit/2024/08/29/detroit-public-school-students-standardized-test-scores/)

## 4. The Money — $288M+/Year Through Charters

### Per-Pupil Funding

Michigan's foundation allowance for FY 2024-25 is **$9,608 per pupil**. With ~30,000
Detroit-resident charter students, that's **~$288M/year in state aid** flowing through
charter schools — before federal and categorical funding.

| Funding Layer | Per Pupil | Total (30,000 students) |
|--------------|-----------|------------------------|
| State foundation only | $9,608 | ~$288M/year |
| Total revenue (incl. federal) | $12,000-$15,000 | ~$360-$450M/year |

### The Authorizer Fee Incentive

Authorizers collect **3% of each school's state aid** — approximately $288 per pupil per year.

| Authorizer | Est. Detroit Portfolio | Est. Annual Fee Revenue |
|-----------|----------------------|------------------------|
| GVSU | ~30 schools, ~8,000 students | ~$2.3M |
| CMU | ~9 schools, ~2,500 students | ~$720K |
| DPSCD | 7 schools, ~1,500 students | ~$430K |
| Others | ~20 schools, ~5,000 students | ~$1.4M |

This creates a **structural conflict of interest**: authorizers are financially
incentivized to authorize more schools and keep them open — regardless of performance.
A university in Grand Rapids collects $2.3M/year for "overseeing" schools it rarely
visits.

### For-Profit CMOs Resist Disclosure

Michigan law requires charter school financial disclosure, but **for-profit management
companies claim private-entity status** and resist detailed financial reporting. When
a for-profit CMO takes 72.67% of a school's revenue (as Purpose Group does with
MacDowell), the public cannot see where that money goes.

The [Detroit Free Press](https://www.freep.com/story/news/local/michigan/2014/06/22/most-michigan-charter-companies-don-t-follow-financial-disclosure-law/77199892/)
documented in 2014 that most Michigan charter companies do not follow financial
disclosure law. The situation has not meaningfully changed.

Source: [Michigan HB 6277 (FY 2024-25 budget)](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/2023-2024/billintroduced/House/pdf/2024-HIB-6277.pdf), [University of Arkansas funding study](http://thestarnewsnetwork.com/2023/08/20/study-detroit-charters-get-6202-less-per-pupil-funding-than-traditional-schools/)

## 5. The Fraud Pattern — Banks Is Not Alone

Charter school financial misconduct in Detroit is not an anomaly. It is a recurring
pattern enabled by structural oversight gaps.

| Case | Issue | Outcome |
|------|-------|---------|
| **Summit Academy** | Insider dealing, kickbacks, contracts to founders' companies | FBI contacted. Cancilliari fired 2013. |
| **University YES Academy** | $300,000 unaccounted Title I funds | High school closed abruptly Aug 2016. |
| **Michigan Technical Academy** | $16M debt; creditors seized state aid | Charter revoked. **Teachers unpaid** for summer work. |
| **Detroit Delta Prep** | Only 1/3 of budget on instruction; $217 reserve fund | Closed **22 days** into the 2018-19 school year. |
| **Old Redford Academy** | $19M bond debt; S&P downgraded to CC | **Closing June 30, 2026**. Default expected. |
| **Creative Montessori** | Double-dipping: state funds + private preschool tuition | Wayne RESA demanded $65K+ refunds. |
| **W-A-Y Academy** | $73,349 unsupported Title I drawdowns | Federal audit finding. |
| **Banks / MacDowell** | 9-time felon as superintendent; 72.67% extraction | Investigation in progress. Second school authorized. |

**The pattern:** An operator receives per-pupil funding → extracts money through
management fees, related-party contracts, or vendor self-dealing → academic outcomes
collapse → school closes or is restructured → **the children are displaced** → the
operator opens a new school.

Banks's dissolve-and-reform pattern (documented in the
[LARA analysis](/analysis/corporate-network-lara/)) mirrors the broader systemic
pattern. Detroit Delta Prep lasted 22 days. Banks's entities dissolve and reform
within months. The children bear the cost each time.

Source: [Detroit Free Press (2014)](https://www.freep.com/story/news/local/michigan/2014/06/23/tangled-web-at-two-charter-schools-shows-shortcomings-of-state/77199894/), [Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/detroit/2018/11/29/21108212/if-we-don-t-learn-from-this-one-shame-on-us-lessons-from-a-detroit-charter-school-that-was-set-up-to/), [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/capital-markets/charter-school-sees-debt-rating-cut-six-notches-as-strain-mounts)

## 6. Special Education — The Hidden Sorting

Charter schools and DPSCD serve similarly poor, majority-Black populations. But they
do not serve the same children.

| Metric | DPSCD | Detroit Charters |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| **Special education enrollment** | **16-18%** | **7-10%** |
| **Students with autism** | **22x more likely** | Rare |
| **Deep poverty (Black students)** | More concentrated | Less concentrated |
| **Overall poverty (FRL)** | ~85% | ~87% |

Children with autism are **22 times more likely** to be in a DPSCD school than a
charter school. Students with severe disabilities are concentrated in DPSCD and
Wayne RESA center programs — not in charters.

This means charter schools receive similar per-pupil funding while serving a
**less costly student population**. The money follows the child, but the most
expensive children stay in the district.

When a charter school reports academic results, those results reflect a
**pre-sorted population** — students whose families chose to enroll them, who
do not have the most severe disabilities, and who are less likely to be in
the deepest poverty. The comparison is not apples to apples.

Source: [Chalkbeat (2018)](https://www.chalkbeat.org/detroit/2018/7/23/21105402/why-detroit-children-with-autism-are-22-times-more-likely-to-be-in-a-district-school-than-a-charter/), [CGCS Detroit Special Education Report](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1757445862/cgcsorg/j2jrvwi3ygzbdb0gvt5c/DETROITSpecialEducationReport--FINALxx.pdf)

## Michigan's Charter Law — National Context

Michigan's charter school law receives widely divergent ratings depending on who
is measuring:

| Framework | Michigan Rank | Assessment |
|-----------|--------------|------------|
| **NAPCS Model Law (2022)** | 30th of 45 states | Weak on authorizer accountability |
| **Center for Education Reform (2024)** | 4th of 45 states | Strong on growth and autonomy |
| **Citizens Research Council (2020)** | Critical | "No statutory oversight standards" |

The gap between these ratings tells the story: Michigan's law is excellent at
**enabling charter school growth** and terrible at **ensuring charter school
accountability**. The law allows rapid expansion (which CER rewards) while providing
no statutory standards for authorizer oversight (which CRC criticizes).

Key Michigan charter law features:
- **No cap** on university-authorized charters (cap expired 2014)
- **No statutory authorizer evaluation system** (only 12 states require this)
- **No explicit oversight standards** — authorizers define their own rigor
- **Weak conflict-of-interest law** — only covers board member relatives (since 2012)
- **For-profit CMOs** can claim private-entity status and resist disclosure
- **2016 Detroit-specific law:** Only accredited authorizers can open new Detroit charters

Source: [NAPCS Model Law (2022)](https://publiccharters.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2022-model-law_rd3.pdf), [CER Scorecard (2024)](https://2024.edreform.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/cer-charterlaws-scorecard-2024_final.pdf), [CRC Report (2020)](https://crcmich.org/wp-content/uploads/rpt409_Charter_School_Oversight-2020-1.pdf)
