## Anderson Localization — The Hemlock Subgraph

In condensed-matter physics, **Anderson localization** describes how
waves — electrons, light, sound — become trapped in disordered
systems. Instead of propagating freely, the wave scatters off
irregularities until it cannot escape its local region. The wave
function is *localized*: it exists, but it goes nowhere.

Accountability works the same way.

In a system with functioning oversight, a complaint propagates:
citizen → agency → investigation → finding → correction. The
wave reaches its destination. The system self-corrects.

In a *localized* system, the complaint scatters off captured
institutions until it dies. The citizen files. The agency dismisses.
The court ignores. The bar association circles. The complaint
exists, but it goes nowhere.

## Why Smaller Subgraphs Are More Dangerous

In a large system (Detroit), corruption requires coordination.
One actor cannot simultaneously be the attorney, the judge, the
board member, the ISP operator, and the FOIA platform. Multiple
people must cooperate, which creates weak links: someone can
flip, someone can talk, someone can be caught.

In a small system (Hemlock), **one family** can hold positions
across every institutional layer. No coordination required. No
co-conspirators to flip. No weak links in the chain.

The Anderson localization is complete. The wave function — every
complaint, every investigation, every FOIA request — is fully
trapped in the local lattice.

## The External Probe

Anderson localization can only be broken by an **external
probe** — a measurement from outside the disordered system.

In physics, this means introducing a new frequency or coupling
the localized system to a larger lattice.

In accountability, this means:

1. **Federal jurisdiction** — agencies outside the local lattice
   (FBI, FTC, DOJ Public Integrity)
2. **Cross-protection** — investigators from other jurisdictions
   documenting the pattern
3. **Publication** — making the architecture visible to anyone
   with an internet connection
4. **Reader investigation** — template FOIAs that any citizen
   can file to test the system's transparency

The localized system cannot see itself. The external probe makes
the localization visible — and once visible, it cannot be
maintained.
