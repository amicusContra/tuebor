## Behaviors Banks Confesses Others Committed

These are not accusations. These are descriptions published by Brian Banks in his own book.

### 1. A Sitting Judge Discussed Active Cases in a Restaurant

> "Judge Langford Morris was describing **her morning docket** to Roeiah and the other young lady and **the various cases that she had heard.**"

Location: Brandy's restaurant, Telegraph Road, Pontiac, Michigan. Approximate date: ~2006-2007 (Banks says "Seven and a half years" after 1999 sentencing).

A sitting Oakland County Circuit Court judge discussed her morning docket — specific cases she heard that same day — with civilians at a restaurant. This violates:
- **Canon 2, Rule 2.10** — Judicial Statements on Pending and Impending Cases
- **Canon 1, Rule 1.2** — Promoting Confidence in the Judiciary
- **MRPC 3.5** — if any attorney was present

### 2. A Sitting Judge Developed a Personal Relationship with a Former Defendant

Banks describes the progression: sentencing → chance reconnection through Roeiah → restaurant lunch → courtroom visits → law school event invitations → graduation dinner → Supreme Court campaign volunteering → book foreword.

This is an improper ex parte personal relationship between a judge and a former felony defendant she sentenced.

### 3. A Sitting Judge Endorsed Credential Fraud via Book Foreword

Langford Morris wrote the foreword to a book branded **"Brian Banks, J.D."** while knowing:
- She sentenced him for multiple felonies
- Those felonies prevented bar admission
- He was using "J.D." as a professional credential without a license

As a **former assistant U.S. Attorney** (Eastern District of Michigan), Langford Morris knows precisely what "J.D." means without bar admission. Her endorsement of the book is an endorsement of the credential fraud.

### 4. "Several Other Judges" Celebrated His "Law Graduation"

> "When I graduated from law school, Judge Langford Morris and **several other judges** and friends treated me to dinner to celebrate."

Multiple unnamed judges personally celebrated a nine-time convicted felon's law school graduation — a defendant whose felonies they knew would prevent bar admission.

### 5. Judges Vonda Evans and Cylenthia Miller Arrived at a Family Hospital

Two sitting judges responded to a personal family medical crisis — Banks's grandfather's hospitalization and death. This is documented personal relationship, not professional interaction.

### 6. A Sitting Judge Had "Felonies and Misdemeanors"

Banks writes about Judge Tenisha Yancey:
> "Tenisha ended up having some troubles of her own which caused her to **spend a month or two in jail**... She then became an **assistant prosecuting attorney even with felonies and misdemeanors.**"

Banks published this about a sitting Wayne County judge. Whether true or false, it is his own party admission about a network member.

### 7. "Prominent People" Knowingly Purchased Stolen Goods

> "Some of the people who were buying the items from us were **prominent people** and they knew that the items were obtained through **illegal means.**"

Banks confesses that unnamed "prominent people" knowingly received stolen merchandise from his organized retail theft operation. He does not name them. They are unindicted co-conspirators described by the defendant himself.

### 8. Gay-Dagnogo and Judge Evans at Hospital Night

A state representative and a sitting judge were at Banks's personal family crisis event. This is the personal network that later voted for PCA's charter (Gay-Dagnogo) and endorsed Banks's campaigns.
