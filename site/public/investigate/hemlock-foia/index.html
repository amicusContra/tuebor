Hemlock FOIA Toolkit — How to Request Records About the Ellison Network

What This Page Is

Ready-to-use FOIA request templates targeting specific Saginaw County agencies. Each template identifies the records, cites the statutory authority, and explains what the responsive documents would reveal.

You do not need a lawyer to file a FOIA request. Michigan's Freedom of Information Act (MCL 15.231 et seq.) gives every person the right to inspect and copy public records. You do not need to state a reason. The agency must respond within 5 business days.

Do not use FOIAworks for any of these requests. See why.

How Michigan FOIA Works

StepWhat HappensStatute
1. Submit requestWritten request identifying the records. Email is acceptable.MCL 15.232(2)
2. Agency respondsWithin 5 business days. Must grant, deny, or request 10-day extension.MCL 15.235(2)
3. If deniedWritten denial with specific exemption cited. You can appeal to agency head.MCL 15.235(4)
4. If appeal deniedYou can file suit in circuit court. Prevailing requesters get attorney's fees.MCL 15.240
5. Fee disputesAgency can charge labor + copying. You can challenge unreasonable fees.MCL 15.234

Tips for Filing

  1. Keep it specific. Name the exact records, entities, and date ranges. Vague requests ("all records about Philip Ellison") give agencies an excuse to delay.

  2. Use email. Michigan courts have held that email is a valid method for submitting FOIA requests. Keep the email as your receipt.

  3. Screenshot the submission. Date-stamped proof that you filed starts the 5-day clock.

  4. If they ask for money up front, you can ask them to narrow the scope or to provide a detailed fee estimate. MCL 15.234 requires the estimate to be itemized.

  5. If they deny, the denial must cite a specific FOIA exemption. "We don't have that" is an acceptable response — if true. "We won't look" is not.

  6. Appeal denials to the head of the public body (superintendent, board chair, county administrator). If the appeal is denied, you can file suit under MCL 15.240.

  7. Document everything. Save every email, every response, every denial. The paper trail IS the evidence.

  8. Share what you find. If you receive responsive documents that reveal conflicts, vendor relationships, or policy failures, you can submit them to this investigation through CONTRIBUTING.md or via hello@clutchjustice.com.
