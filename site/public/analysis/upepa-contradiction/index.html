The UPEPA Contradiction — How One Attorney Broke Michigan's Anti-SLAPP Statute

Michigan Finally Got an Anti-SLAPP Law. Then This Happened.

On March 24, 2026, Michigan's Uniform Public Expression Protection Act (UPEPA, 2025 PA 52) took effect. It was designed to protect citizens from strategic lawsuits targeting their rights to public expression — commonly known as SLAPP suits.

Two days later, Philip L. Ellison filed the first UPEPA motion in Saginaw County.

He filed it to protect himself — while simultaneously maintaining a SLAPP suit against a journalist.

What UPEPA Does

ProtectionStatuteEffect
Expedited dismissalMCL 691.1853Defendant can move to dismiss within 60 days of service
Automatic stayMCL 691.1854(1)(a)All proceedings automatically halt when the motion is filed — non-discretionary
Burden shiftMCL 691.1856Moving party establishes prima facie case of protected expression → burden shifts to plaintiff to show merit
Mandatory fee-shiftingMCL 691.1860Prevailing defendant gets costs + attorney's fees. Non-discretionary.
Additional sanctionsMCL 691.1860Court may award additional sanctions beyond fees

The purpose, stated in MCL 691.1851(2)(b): to "protect citizens from the chilling effect of retributive and abusive strategic lawsuits against public participation."

Source: Michigan Legislature — Act 52 of 2025 · Full text (PDF)

The Judicial Estoppel Argument

The doctrine of judicial estoppel prevents a party from taking a legal position that contradicts one they successfully argued in a prior proceeding.

Ellison argued — twice, in filed motions — that UPEPA applies to post-effective-date claims in pre-effective-date lawsuits. That is the identical legal argument the journalist's UPEPA motion advances. He cannot argue against it without contradicting his own filings.

As Rita Williams documented in Clutch Justice, May 7, 2026:

"He cannot argue a legal position in one proceeding and then argue its opposite in another. That is the doctrine. He is bound by his own filings."

Source: Clutch Justice, May 1, 2026 · Clutch Justice, May 7, 2026

The Same Judge Problem

Both cases are before Judge Julie A. Gafkay.

She holds both sides of the judicial estoppel argument:

  • In Case 1 (Consumers Energy), Ellison argued UPEPA applies retroactively. Her court denied the motion as moot — meaning she never ruled on the legal argument.
  • In Case 2 (the SLAPP), the journalist argues UPEPA applies using the same logic. Ellison opposes.

If she rules UPEPA applies in Case 2, it vindicates the position Ellison took in Case 1. If she rules it doesn't apply, she undermines his filed position in Case 1. Either way, ruling on one case creates precedent that governs the other — and the same attorney is on opposite sides.

This is a structural problem that a single-judge court in a mid-size county is not designed to handle.

Why This Is UPEPA's First Real Test

Michigan's anti-SLAPP statute has been in effect since March 24, 2026. It is less than seven months old. There is essentially no Michigan case law interpreting it.

The Saginaw County cases may produce the first published opinions on:

  1. Retroactivity — Does UPEPA apply to causes of action asserted after March 24 in lawsuits filed before?
  2. Judicial estoppel in UPEPA context — Can an attorney invoke UPEPA as a shield and oppose it as a sword in the same court?
  3. Automatic stay enforcement — MCL 691.1854(1)(a) says proceedings halt automatically. Does the trial court have discretion to proceed anyway?
  4. Fee-shifting after void litigation — If the SLAPP suit was void ab initio (bankruptcy stay violation), does UPEPA fee-shifting still apply?

These are questions of first impression. The answers will determine whether UPEPA is a meaningful protection or a paper statute that attorneys can manipulate by taking contradictory positions in parallel cases.

The Structural Vulnerability

UPEPA was designed to protect the journalist. In practice:

Design IntentReality
Expedited dismissal within 60 daysCase has been pending since November 2025 — 11 months
Automatic stay upon filingUPEPA motion filed May 6, 2026; proceedings continued
Mandatory fee-shifting for prevailing defendantCannot prevail until the court rules on the motion
Protection from retaliatory litigationThe attorney taking contradictory UPEPA positions is the same attorney who filed the SLAPP

The statute provides strong tools. The enforcement depends on a court that is willing to use them against a local attorney who appears before it in three simultaneous cases, whose wife presides over the county's education system, and who is a colleague of the judge at the same university.

The statute is structural. The enforcement is personal. That gap is where UPEPA breaks.

What UPEPA Needs To Work

  1. Mandatory timelines with consequences. The statute says "expedited." It does not define what happens when the court misses the timeline.

  2. Venue transfer for serial SLAPP filers. When an attorney files a SLAPP suit in the same court where they have multiple other cases, the structural conflict is inherent. Transfer to a neutral venue removes the pressure.

  3. AGC referral trigger. If a UPEPA motion succeeds, the court should be required to refer the SLAPP filer to the AGC for evaluation under MRPC 3.1 (meritorious claims). Currently, the AGC only receives referrals that someone manually files.

  4. Cross-case tracking. The court system does not automatically flag when the same attorney takes contradictory legal positions in parallel cases. SCAO could build this — it chooses not to.
