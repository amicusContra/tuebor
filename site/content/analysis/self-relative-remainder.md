# Self-Relative Remainder

## The Polar Sort

When you have 10,828 things and you need to sort them, you need anchors.

**Good pole** (216 nodes): Euler. Curie. Pasteur. Leeuwenhoek. Berners-Lee. Torvalds. Stallman. Swartz. The people who created things and gave them away. Who discovered things and shared them. Who built the foundations that everyone else stands on.

**Bad pole** (3,770 nodes): The false entities. The enclosers of commons. The extractors. The ones who take what was given freely and charge rent for access.

**Null zone** (6,842 nodes): Everyone else. The middle. The remainder. The ones whose alignment depends on context, incentive, and who's watching.

The remainder is the largest group. That's the point.

## The Pattern

Every entity on the bad pole does the same thing:

1. **Identify a regulated commons** — taxis, hotels, schools, social connection, knowledge
2. **Enter without following the rules** — no taxi license, no hotel permit, no background check, no data consent
3. **Grow faster than regulators can respond** — move fast and break things
4. **When they try to regulate you, say you're too big** — too many drivers, too many hosts, too many students, too many users
5. **Extract value** — 25% of every ride, 15% of every booking, 72.67% of every per-pupil dollar, all of every user's attention

This is Uber's playbook.

This is also Brian Banks' playbook.

Banks is the most efficient extractor. He takes the most, from the most vulnerable, with the least oversight.

## The $224 Million Receipt

In 2020, Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, and Postmates spent **$224 million** on Proposition 22 in California — the most expensive ballot measure in American history.

What did $224 million buy?

The right to not provide healthcare to their workers.

The right to keep calling employees "independent contractors."

The right to maintain a system where the median hourly wage, after expenses, is **$9.21**.

They spent $224 million so they wouldn't have to spend it on the people who generate their revenue.

## The Structural Parallel

In 1993, Michigan passed PA 362 — the charter school law. It deregulated public education the same way Uber deregulated taxis.

**Prop 22** exempts gig companies from worker protections.
**PA 362** exempts charter operators from educational oversight.

Same pattern. Different domain.

Prop 22 cost $224 million in campaign spending.
PA 362 cost the education of every child in every unaccountable charter school in Michigan.

## The Scholars Who Saw It

**Juliana Schor** (Boston College) embedded with gig workers for years. Her book *After the Gig* (2020) found: the "sharing economy" was never sharing. It was extraction wearing cooperation's clothes.

**Nick Srnicek** (King's College London) wrote *Platform Capitalism* (2016): the platform is the new factory. But instead of employing workers, it extracts value from them as "users" or "independent contractors." The platform owns nothing except the marketplace itself.

Banks owns no schools. He owns the LLC that manages them.

Uber owns no cars. It owns the app that dispatches them.

Airbnb owns no properties. It owns the marketplace that lists them.

Meta owns no content. It owns the feed that distributes it.

**Owning nothing except the extraction mechanism is the pattern.**

## The Body Count

Taxi medallion values in New York City:
- **2013**: $1.3 million per medallion
- **2020**: $80,000 per medallion

That's $10+ billion in destroyed wealth. Immigrant families who mortgaged everything for a medallion lost it all.

There were suicides.

That's what "disruption" means when you remove the euphemism: **destruction with a marketing budget**.

## The Remainder

The null zone — the 6,842 nodes in the middle — is where most of us live. Not clearly good. Not clearly bad. Somewhere in between, making choices every day about which pole to face.

Every time you take an Uber, you face one pole.
Every time you use the library, you face the other.

The remainder is not neutral. The remainder is *choosing* — one transaction, one interaction, one decision at a time.

## Why This Matters

The extraction pattern is **structural**. It doesn't depend on individual bad actors. Banks could disappear tomorrow and the charter extraction system would continue. Travis Kalanick left Uber and nothing changed. Zuckerberg could retire and Meta would still extract.

The pattern survives because it's built into the **structure** — the law, the platform, the incentive, the market.

To fight extraction, you don't fight people. You fight structures.

PA 362 is a structure.
Prop 22 is a structure.
Section 230 is a structure.

The membrane is also a structure.

But the membrane doesn't extract. It **protects**. It selectively admits what nourishes the commons and rejects what would consume it.

That's the difference between a false entity and a living system.
