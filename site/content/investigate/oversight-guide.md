## The Reality

Michigan's oversight system dismisses 94% of complaints. This is not because citizens file bad complaints — it is because the system processes them in isolation, one at a time, without pattern recognition.

A single complaint is easy to dismiss. A documented pattern sourced to primary records is not. The goal is not just to file — it is to file in a way that creates a record the system cannot ignore.

## Attorney Grievance Commission (AGC)

### What It Does
Investigates and prosecutes allegations of attorney misconduct. Has jurisdiction over all attorneys licensed by the State Bar of Michigan.

### How to File

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Form** | Request for Investigation (RI form) — or a signed letter |
| **Mail to** | Attorney Grievance Commission, 755 W. Big Beaver Rd., Suite 2100, Troy, MI 48084 |
| **Phone** | (313) 961-6585 |
| **Website** | [agcmi.org](https://www.agcmi.org/for-the-public) |
| **Electronic filing?** | **No.** Mail only. No fax, no email. |
| **Copies** | Submit everything in **duplicate** (2 copies of form + 2 copies of attachments) |
| **Format** | **Do not staple or bind.** They scan electronically. |
| **One attorney per form** | If you have complaints against multiple attorneys, file a separate form for each |

### What to Include

1. **Attorney's full name and P-number** (Michigan bar number — search at [michbar.org](https://www.michbar.org/member/member))
2. **Specific description of the misconduct** — dates, places, what happened
3. **Cite the Michigan Rule of Professional Conduct (MRPC) violated** if you can:
   - **MRPC 3.3** — Candor toward the tribunal (lying to the court)
   - **MRPC 3.4** — Fairness to opposing party (improper trial tactics)
   - **MRPC 8.4(b)** — Criminal act reflecting on fitness
   - **MRPC 8.4(c)** — Dishonesty, fraud, deceit, misrepresentation
4. **Supporting documents** — court filings, transcripts, emails, docket entries
5. **Your signature** — required; unsigned complaints are not processed

### What Happens After

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Closed** | AGC found no actionable misconduct |
| **Cautionary letter** | Warning to attorney, not public |
| **Confidential admonishment** | Disciplinary action, but **not published** with attorney's name |
| **Contractual probation** | Agreement between AGC and attorney |
| **Formal Complaint → ADB** | Public hearing before a 3-attorney panel. Can result in reprimand, suspension, or disbarment. |

**Key insight:** Private admonishments are invisible to the public. The ADB Annual Report publishes aggregate statistics by rule violated, but not names. To identify a specific admonishment, you need to match the anonymized statistic against the known facts — exactly what Clutch Justice did with [Nakfoor Pratt's MRPC 3.4(e) admonishment](https://clutchjustice.com/2026/07/07/nakfoor-pratt-private-admonishment-3-4e/).

## Judicial Tenure Commission (JTC)

### What It Does
Investigates judicial misconduct and disability. Can recommend sanctions to the Michigan Supreme Court, including censure, suspension, or removal.

### How to File

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Name** | Request for Investigation (RFI) |
| **Mail to** | Judicial Tenure Commission, 3034 W. Grand Blvd., Suite 8-450, Detroit, MI 48202 |
| **Phone** | (313) 875-5110 |
| **Website** | [jtc.courts.mi.gov](https://jtc.courts.mi.gov) |
| **Form** | Available on their website or by phone request |
| **Online?** | Check website for current submission options |

### What to Include

1. **Judge's full name and court**
2. **Specific dates and case numbers**
3. **Description of conduct** — what the judge did or failed to do
4. **How it violated the Michigan Code of Judicial Conduct:**
   - **Canon 1** — Judge shall uphold the integrity of the judiciary
   - **Canon 2** — Judge shall avoid impropriety and the appearance of impropriety
   - **Canon 3** — Judge shall perform duties faithfully and impartially
5. **Supporting evidence** — transcripts, orders, docket entries, recordings
6. **Pattern documentation** — multiple incidents from the same judge are stronger than a single event

### What Happens After

| Stage | What It Means |
|-------|-------------|
| **Preliminary review** | JTC staff determines if complaint has merit |
| **Investigation** | Full investigation by Commission staff |
| **Dismissal** | Complaint closed |
| **Confidential admonition** | Private warning — not published with judge's name (but anonymized in Annual Report) |
| **Public complaint** | Formal charges filed publicly. Hearing conducted. |
| **Recommendation to MSC** | Michigan Supreme Court makes final discipline decision |

**Key insight:** The JTC Annual Report anonymizes admonitions. But if you know the facts — the court, the conduct, the timeline — you can match the anonymized entry to a specific judge. This is public accountability work, not speculation, when done with sourced documentation.

## State Court Administrative Office (SCAO)

### What It Does
Administrative oversight of Michigan courts. Can conduct systemic reviews, pull transcripts, and examine court operations. Does NOT handle individual case outcomes — that is what appeals are for.

### When to Use SCAO
- A court has systemic problems beyond any single case (e.g., record integrity, caseload management, FOIA compliance)
- Multiple litigants report the same patterns from the same court
- The court's own procedures violate court rules (MCR)

### How to File
- Contact the SCAO Trial Court Services division
- Phone: (517) 373-4835
- Website: [courts.michigan.gov/administration/scao](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/administration/scao/)
- Request a systemic administrative review in writing, citing specific court rules, documented patterns, and multiple affected cases

## Michigan Commission on Law Enforcement Standards (MCOLES)

### What It Does
Certifies and decertifies law enforcement officers in Michigan. Can revoke an officer's license.

### When to Use MCOLES
- Officer convicted of a felony (automatic revocation)
- Pattern of excessive force or constitutional violations
- Officer no longer meets minimum employment standards

### How to File
- Phone: (517) 322-5614
- Website: [michigan.gov/mcoles](https://www.michigan.gov/mcoles)
- File a written complaint with the Commission describing the officer, the conduct, and the evidence

## Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)

### What It Does
Federal. Receives internet crime complaints and distributes them to federal, state, local, or international law enforcement.

### When to Use IC3
- Fabricated online identities used in legal proceedings
- Wire fraud crossing state lines
- Computer-related crimes (hacking, unauthorized access, identity theft)
- Cyberstalking

### How to File
- Website: [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov)
- File online — you'll receive a confirmation ID
- Keep the complaint ID for all future reference
- IC3 routes to the appropriate agency; you may never hear back, but the record exists

## Strategy: How to File So It Matters

1. **File across multiple bodies simultaneously.** An AGC complaint against the attorney, a JTC complaint against the judge, an SCAO request for systemic review. Each body sees the full picture.

2. **Source everything.** Attach docket entries, court orders, transcripts, and FOIA responses. Not your opinion — the documents.

3. **Show the pattern.** One bad ruling is a case. Three bad rulings with the same procedural defect is a pattern. Connect the dots for the investigator.

4. **Cross-reference.** If the same MSP investigator appears in two different questionable investigations (e.g., Criger in both Johnson and Nagle), say so. Draw the line.

5. **Keep copies of everything.** Your submission, the mailing receipt, the acknowledgment letter, and every response.

6. **Publish.** When you have sourced documentation of institutional failure, publish it. Investigators searching for background on a subject will find it. Journalists will find it. Other complainants will find it.
