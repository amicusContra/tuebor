The [Michigan Online Educator Certification System (MOECS)](https://mdoe.state.mi.us/MOECS/PublicCredentialSearch.aspx) is the public database where anyone can verify whether a Michigan educator holds a valid teaching certificate, administrator certification, or permit — and which grade levels and subjects each endorsement covers. {{ confidence(level="documented") }}

On **September 26, 2026**, every name listed on MacDowell Preparatory Academy's public staff roster ([macdowellprep.com/about/](https://www.macdowellprep.com/about/)) was searched in MOECS. The audit found that **only 2 of 14 instructional staff members** are properly credentialed **and** correctly placed for their teaching assignment. {{ confidence(level="documented") }}

## The Rachel Webb Finding {{ confidence(level="documented") }}

> **A kindergarten teacher holds a Secondary English/Journalism certification endorsed for grades 6–12.**
>
> Elementary education in Michigan requires a specific endorsement in early childhood or elementary education. A 6–12 secondary certification does **not** qualify an educator for K–5 instruction under Michigan law. MOECS shows Rachel Webb's only endorsements are **English 6–12** and **Journalism 6–12** — neither covers kindergarten.

Daily Substitute Permits are tied to the **issuing school** and are intended for **temporary fill-in duty**, not permanent full-time instructional positions. Using substitute permits from other schools to staff ongoing teaching roles is a credential compliance issue under Michigan law.

Volanda Frazier holds a Full-Year Basic Substitute permit as the full-time 8th grade Teacher of Record — a separate but related compliance problem.

## The Law — MCL 388.1763 {{ confidence(level="documented") }}

Michigan Compiled Laws require charter school teachers to hold valid credentials:

> *"A teacher employed by or assigned to a public school academy shall hold a valid Michigan teaching certificate or a permit issued by the superintendent of public instruction."*

**Penalty provisions when violations are found:**

| Statute | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| **MCL 388.1763(1)** | A district shall not employ an individual who is not appropriately placed under a valid certificate. |
| **MCL 388.1763(2)** | **50% state aid deduction** for salary paid during the violation period. |
| **MCL 388.1763(6)** | If a school official is **notified** by MDE and **knowingly continues** to employ an uncredentialed individual → **misdemeanor**, **$1,500 fine per incident**. |

When MDE identifies credential violations, the statute requires enforcement — state aid deductions for uncredentialed employment and, where officials continue employment after notification, referral for criminal prosecution.

## What MDE Did {{ confidence(level="documented") }}

MDE was notified of credential problems at MacDowell. Over a **four-year period**, the Office of Educator Excellence investigated **only one name** — Brian Banks — in February 2022. The investigation was **closed within approximately two hours** after OEE Analyst **Katie Schmiedeknecht** communicated that "there will be no need for an investigation."

MDE **never checked the rest of the staff roster** — despite MOECS and the Educator Registry (REP) being MDE's own systems, searchable by any analyst in minutes.

Schmiedeknecht presents at state conferences on the **same credential compliance rules** she is charged with enforcing — including slides stating that *"Any teacher who is not appropriately placed may be subject to a salary deduction based on MCL 388.1763."* Her office failed to enforce those rules at MacDowell while advising the school through the compliance loophole that avoided penalties for Banks.

*Source: MDE FOIA response (September 25, 2026); MOECS public search (September 26, 2026).*
