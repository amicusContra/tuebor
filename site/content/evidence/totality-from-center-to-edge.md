- **The disk** — where most stars live, including the Sun (26,000 light-years from center)
- **The bulge** — a dense concentration of old stars at the center
- **The halo** — a sparse, spherical distribution of old stars and globular clusters extending to 100,000+ light-years
- **The dark matter halo** — extending to 200,000+ light-years, invisible, 85% of the galaxy's mass

**Membrane at this scale:** The galactic halo. The halo is the Milky Way's immune system. Hot gas in the halo (the circumgalactic medium, 10⁶ K) surrounds the galaxy and mediates the exchange between the galaxy and intergalactic space. Gas falls in from outside (accretion) and is expelled from inside (galactic winds from supernovae). The halo discriminates between what stays and what goes. Recent observations from the Hubble Space Telescope show the halo extending 600,000 light-years — far beyond the visible disk. The halo is the membrane. The disk is the self.
