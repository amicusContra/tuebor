Institutional Immunity Pattern

The Numbers

Michigan's attorney discipline system dismisses the vast majority of complaints. The AGC's own annual reports document this:

Source: AGC 2024 Annual Report (PDF) · AGC 2023 Annual Report (PDF) · Michigan Bar Journal, grievance confidentiality primer — citing 15,816/21,048 (2012–2020)

The system's headline rate depends on how you count. By the AGC's narrowest measure (rejections at GA intake), roughly 60% are dismissed before anyone investigates. By the broader measure that includes Commission closures, the rate exceeds 88%. By the nine-year longitudinal measure, three of every four complaints are dismissed by the Grievance Administrator before reaching a hearing panel.

As Clutch Justice documented on September 24, 2026: attorneys who regularly appear before specific Michigan judges have independently described the same pattern — hearings logged as status conferences, months without written rulings, appearances that accomplish nothing procedurally — across counties, over years.

In 2025, 99% of resolved judicial grievances closed without any finding of misconduct. Only five became public complaints. Fourteen received confidential discipline — their names never released.

As the Detroit Free Press reported (Aug 10, 2026): offenses included negligence leading to wrongful arrest, delaying cases for years, and mistreating court staff — all handled behind closed doors.

2025's 698 grievances was a 32% increase over 2024 (530), the largest single-year jump in a decade. The JTC speculated publicity around existing public complaints drove awareness. Clutch Justice analyzed the 2025 report in detail.

The AGC and JTC together process over 3,000 complaints per year. Fewer than 100 result in any public consequence. The system does not fail to receive complaints. It fails to act on them.

This does not mean all dismissed complaints are meritorious. It means the system is structured to evaluate each complaint in isolation, without pattern recognition, and to resolve the vast majority through confidential dispositions that leave no public record.

How Isolation Creates Immunity

The Single-Complaint Model

Complainant A files about Attorney X → Investigated alone → Dismissed
Complainant B files about Attorney X → Investigated alone → Dismissed
Complainant C files about Attorney X → Investigated alone → Private admonishment

No investigator sees that three different people complained about the same attorney for the same conduct. Each file is closed before the next one arrives, or they sit in separate staff queues without cross-referencing.

The Confidential Disposition Model

When the system does act, it often acts confidentially:

  • Private admonishment (AGC) — not published with attorney's name
  • Confidential admonition (JTC) — not published with judge's name
  • Cautionary letter — no public record at all

The public cannot verify whether an attorney or judge has been disciplined. The next complainant does not know the system already found a problem.

The Annual Report Workaround

The ADB and JTC publish aggregate statistics in annual reports — how many complaints by rule violated, how many private admonishments by type. But they do not attach names.

This creates a forensic opportunity: if you know the facts — the court, the conduct, the timeline — you can sometimes match the anonymized statistic to a specific person. Clutch Justice did this with:

  • Nakfoor Pratt — matched the single MRPC 3.4(e) private admonishment in the ADB 2021 Annual Report to the Bartlett complaint
  • Galen — matched the anonymized JTC 2024 admonition to the Cork Wine Pub facts

This is not speculation. It is pattern analysis against public records. The same method is available to anyone who reads the annual reports against the documented facts.

The Cross-Reference Fix

The fix is not more complaints. It is cross-referenced complaints:

Instead of...Do this...
Filing one AGC complaintFile the AGC complaint AND a JTC complaint against the judge who enabled it AND an SCAO request for systemic review of the court
Describing one incidentDocument the pattern: here are three incidents from three different complainants showing the same procedural defect
Accepting a private resolutionPublish the facts. The confidential admonishment exists. The annual report statistic exists. The matching facts exist. Let the public connect them.
Waiting for the system to actBuild the record publicly. When investigators search for a subject's name, they should find your documentation.

What "No One Is Coming" Actually Means

It does not mean no one cares. It means the system is not built to find patterns. It processes individual complaints through individual investigators who do not routinely check whether other complaints about the same subject exist.

You can build the pattern yourself. This site is one example. The detroit.primals.eco site is another. The Clutch Justice archive is a third. Each one maps a different piece of the same institutional failure. Together, they create the cross-reference the oversight system does not.

The Mathematics

If the AGC receives 2,500 complaints per year (approximate) and dismisses 94%:

  • 2,350 are dismissed or confidentially resolved
  • 150 result in some form of visible action
  • Of those 150, many are consent dispositions or reprimands
  • Actual suspensions and disbarments: a fraction of 150

For judges, the JTC's numbers are smaller but the dynamic is the same:

  • Hundreds of RFIs per year
  • Most dismissed at preliminary review
  • A handful of confidential admonitions
  • Public complaints: single digits per year
  • Removal: essentially never

The institutional incentive is to close files, not to find patterns.

The Counter-Strategy

  1. Publish sourced documentation where investigators can find it
  2. Cross-reference — show the same actor, the same MSP team, the same oversight failure across multiple cases
  3. File simultaneously across multiple oversight bodies — make the system see the same facts from multiple angles
  4. Use the annual reports — match anonymized outcomes to documented facts
  5. Build the network — when multiple affected people document independently, the pattern becomes undeniable

That is what Tuebor is for.
