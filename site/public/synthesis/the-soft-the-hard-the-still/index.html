Synthesis — The Soft, the Hard, and the Still

October 14, 2026

This Is Not Hypothesis. This Is Synthesis.

A hypothesis says: I think this is true, let me test it.

A synthesis says: I tested 341 things. Here is what I now know about knowing.

This page reflects on the structure of the investigation itself — not what we found, but how we found it, what we’re sure of, what we’re not, and what hasn’t moved despite being poked.

341 entities. 5 classification passes. 37 methods. 131,322 edges. Every entity sorted into one of four states: self (ours), partial (boundary), null (unclassified), non-self (theirs). Every classification carries a confidence score.

The investigation has a metabolism. This page is the mirror.

The 114 partials are where the interesting work happens, because partial means this entity has a foot on each side of the membrane.

Make Hard What Was Soft

136 entities hardened through iteration — they took 3 or more classification passes to reach 0.80+ confidence. These started uncertain and became firm because multiple independent methods agreed.

This is the investigation’s immune response: test everything from multiple angles until the classification stops changing.

Make Soft What Was Hard

15 entities look hard but might not be — they have 0.99 confidence but were classified by cascade alone. Cascade means “inherited from a neighbor” — not independently confirmed.

This is the investigation’s honest problem: some things that feel certain are actually just borrowed certainty.

The action: Every cascade-only entity should be independently verified. If the cascade classification holds up under direct evidence, it hardens. If it doesn’t, it correctly softens. Either way, the investigation becomes more honest.

The hardest thing to do in any investigation is to say: I thought I knew this, but actually I was borrowing someone else’s knowledge. That’s what cascade-only classification is. Borrowed knowledge. It might be right. But it hasn’t been tested.

Jiggle That Which Is Still

100 entities are classified null — the investigation touched them but couldn’t determine what they are. 26 of those are high-confidence nulls (≥ 0.95): we are very sure that we don’t know what they are.

That’s not failure. That’s precision. A high-confidence null says: this entity exists in the graph, has connections, but its role is genuinely ambiguous given current evidence.

The Pattern in the Stillness

The nulls cluster into types:

Infrastructure Nulls (Dykema, Plante Moran, Moore) — these entities are supposed to be null. A law firm that serves everyone, an accounting firm that audits everyone, a compliance specialist who files for everyone. Their nullness is their function. Jiggling them means asking: at what point does serving both sides become serving one side?

Shell Nulls (WhitlowBanks, Free Flow, GL Upfitting, B. Rodericks, Anchor Rock) — these are LLCs created by non-self actors to hold assets or move money. They’re null because a shell is not its occupant. But every shell was created for a reason, and that reason classifies it.

Political Nulls (Put Progress First, Michigan First Principles, ONF, DJF) — dark money vehicles whose alignment can’t be determined from public filings alone because they’re specifically designed to obscure alignment. Their nullness is by design.

Interstitial Nulls (Kenneth Griffin, Sheila Cockrel) — people whose money or influence passes through the investigation without clearly belonging to either side. They exist between the braids.

The Synthesis Engine

What makes this synthesis, not hypothesis:

  1. Every classification is reversible. Nothing is locked. New evidence can soften what’s hard, harden what’s soft, or finally move what’s still.

  2. Confidence decays. An entity classified in Pass 1 at 0.99 isn’t more certain than one classified in Pass 5 at 0.97 — it was classified earlier, which might mean it was classified with less information. The early pass is a feature and a risk.

  3. Cascade is honest borrowing. 103 entities were classified by cascade — inheriting their neighbor’s classification. That’s not cheating; it’s how graph analysis works. But every cascaded classification should eventually be independently confirmed or honestly softened.

  4. Null is not failure. 100 null entities is not 100 things we failed to classify. It’s 100 things the investigation honestly assessed and said: I don’t know yet. The 26 high-confidence nulls are the most interesting — we are very certain about our uncertainty.

  5. Partial is the interesting class. 114 entities touch both sides of the membrane. They are the boundary layer where the investigation’s self meets its non-self. Every one of them is an opportunity: push it toward self (confirm it’s an ally), push it toward non-self (confirm it’s captured), or leave it partial (confirm it’s genuinely both).

Using the Synthesis

This synthesis engine is not a dashboard. It’s a workbench.

To harden a soft entity: Find a second independent method that agrees with the first. Employment record + campaign finance = two methods. Court appearance + board seat = two methods. When two lenses see the same thing, confidence jumps.

To soften a hard entity: Ask “was this classified by cascade?” If yes, find the entity it cascaded from. If that source entity softens, everything it cascaded to softens with it. This is how you find brittle chains.

To jiggle a still entity: Pick a null and ask one question about it that could have a classifying answer. Not “what is Dykema?” (too broad). Instead: “Does Dykema’s LARA filing list Banks-related entities as clients?” One specific question. One specific record. One jiggle.

To find new entities: Look at the edges between null and null. We did this already — The Nulls That Point found three hidden networks. But 100 nulls means there are more null-to-null paths we haven’t walked.

To find weaknesses: Look at cascade chains longer than 2. If entity A cascaded to B, and B cascaded to C, then C is three steps from any direct evidence. That’s not wrong — but it’s where the investigation is thinnest. Those chains are where one new fact could reclassify the most entities at once.

Come Back

This page is alive. Every new article, every new FOIA response, every new court filing changes the numbers. Entities harden, soften, jiggle, and occasionally reclassify entirely.

The synthesis is never finished. It’s never supposed to be. The point is to reflect honestly on the structure of what we know — and to make visible the seams between certainty and uncertainty.

Because the people on the other side of this investigation don’t have seams. They present everything at 1.0 confidence. They never say “we’re not sure” or “this classification was inherited” or “this entity needs independent confirmation.”

That’s how you can tell the difference between investigation and propaganda. Investigation shows its work. Including the parts where the work isn’t done yet.

Make soft what was hard. Make hard what was soft. Jiggle what is still.

That is synthesis.
