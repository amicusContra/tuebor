## The Docket Patterns

### Pattern 1: Same Facts, Multiple Cases

The **Consumers Energy tree-marking** generated at least **three** separate cases from the same facts:

1. **Sova v. Consumers Energy** (25-002533-CH) — class action, Saginaw County
2. **Ellison v. Scripps** (1:26-cv-12029) — personal § 1983 federal suit
3. **Engel v. Michigan PSC** (COA 379742) — MPSC jurisdiction challenge

All three involve the same blue-paint tree marking in Saginaw County. All three ultimately land before **the same judge**: Julie A. Gafkay at the 10th Circuit level. The federal case was filed AFTER the Saginaw County case was dismissed by Gafkay.

### Pattern 2: UPEPA as Both Shield and Sword

| Case | UPEPA Role | Position |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Sova/Ellison v. Consumers Energy | **Shield** — Ellison invoked UPEPA to dismiss Consumers' counterclaim | "UPEPA applies post-effective-date" |
| OLC v. Williams | **Sword** — Ellison opposes Williams' UPEPA motion to dismiss his SLAPP suit | Position unknown but contradicts shield use |

Judicial estoppel: the same attorney cannot take opposite positions on the same statute before the same judge.

This is not improper — § 1988 fee-shifting is designed to incentivize civil rights enforcement regardless of monetary damages. But the architecture explains the economic engine: the clients are vehicles for the fee petitions.

## The OLC v. Treasury Irony

On **July 11, 2025**, the Michigan Court of Appeals affirmed dismissal of Ellison's own FOIA case against the Department of Treasury because his verification was **not signed before an officer authorized to administer oaths** — a basic procedural requirement under MCL 600.6431(2)(d).

The same attorney who:
- Operates [FOIAworks](https://foiaworks.com) — charging $4.95–$69.95/month for FOIA filing
- Markets himself as Michigan's preeminent FOIA enforcement attorney
- Won FOIA cases against Oakley ($18,615 fees) and University of Michigan
- Sued FOIA coordinators in at least 8 Michigan counties

...had his own FOIA case thrown out for failing to get his complaint notarized.

**Source:** [COA 374178, Outside Legal Counsel PLC v. Department of Treasury](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/appeals/2025/071125/83986.pdf), published opinion, July 11, 2025.
