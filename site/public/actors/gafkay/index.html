Why This Matters

She cannot rule against Ellison in Case 1 (the SLAPP) without implications for her assessment of his credibility, competence, and legal positions in Cases 2 and 3.

She cannot rule on Ellison’s UPEPA motion in Case 2 (Ellison v. Consumers) without creating the precedent that governs Rita’s UPEPA motion in Case 1 (the SLAPP). The same judge holds both sides of the judicial estoppel argument.

She cannot evaluate Ellison’s fitness as class counsel in Case 3 (Sova v. Consumers — a case affecting potentially thousands of Saginaw County residents) while simultaneously adjudicating evidence that he fabricated a witness, misrepresented an AGC investigation, and filed a SLAPP suit against a journalist in a case that was void from filing.

The structural incentive is to keep all three cases moving without confronting the pattern — because confronting it in one case destabilizes the other two.

What Rita Has Documented

Per Clutch Justice, August 3, 2026:

At the August 3, 2026 show cause hearing before Judge Gafkay, Ellison stated on the record:

  1. That his client asked him for filings and records concerning Rita Williams
  2. That his client “was interested”
  3. That he provided them
  4. That he was “very positive” the same client created the domains threatening Rita and her minor child

The client (Lindke) is not a party to the SLAPP suit. Rule 1.4 (keeping clients informed) applies to a client’s own case, not transmitting documents from one case to a non-party in another. The stated reason — “interest” — is not a litigation purpose.

Judge Gafkay heard all of this on the record. The UPEPA motion and sanctions motion remain pending.

The Question

This is not an accusation of corruption. It is a question of structural judicial conflict.

Can a judge fairly adjudicate a SLAPP suit against a journalist when:

  1. She was the adjunct faculty colleague of the plaintiff/attorney for at least six years?
  2. She simultaneously presides over two other active cases where the same attorney is her lead litigant?
  3. She holds both sides of a judicial estoppel argument (his contradictory UPEPA positions)?
  4. She heard on the record that the attorney’s client created domains targeting a child and received court filings about the journalist for the purpose of “interest” — and the motions remain pending?
  5. Ruling against the attorney in Case 1 could destabilize Cases 2 and 3?

MCR 2.003(C)(1) provides for disqualification when a judge’s impartiality “might reasonably be questioned.” The question is not whether Gafkay is biased — it is whether a reasonable observer, knowing these facts, would question whether she can be impartial.
