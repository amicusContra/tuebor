The Contempt Pattern

Borrello signed a show cause order for contempt in a defamation case where the defendant is a journalist exercising protected expression. The show cause was filed before any adjudication on the merits of the defamation claim and before the UPEPA special motion was heard.

The sequence:

  1. Attorney files SLAPP suit against journalist
  2. Attorney submits fabricated witness ("Samantha Aljouny")
  3. Court issues contempt order against the journalist
  4. Journalist files emergency appeal (COA 380599)
  5. Judge denies disqualification motion
  6. Judge immediately recuses himself anyway

The recusal — voluntarily, on the same day as denying the mandatory basis for disqualification — is itself a documented event. It suggests the judge recognized the appearance problem while declining to rule it met the mandatory standard.

What the UPEPA Motion Inherits

The successor judge receives:

  • The pending UPEPA special motion for expedited relief
  • The full record including the fabricated witness evidence
  • The show cause contempt proceedings
  • The Court of Appeals emergency application (COA 380599)
  • The Motion for Sanctions designating six frivolous filings

A UPEPA ruling has not been made. The statute mandates expedited hearing and provides for fee-shifting when the motion succeeds.

Other Documented Rulings

Borrello has served as a Saginaw County Circuit Court judge for years. Documented rulings include:

  • Homicide trial (2017): Declared mistrial after hung jury in the murder of 16-year-old Walter Riley III
  • Forgery case: Sentenced former Saginaw council candidate to 18 months probation and 140 hours community service
  • Child death case (2025): Sentenced defendant to 6.5–15 and 6.5–10 years concurrent for involuntary manslaughter and second-degree child abuse

These are included for completeness, not comparison. The relevant question is whether the court's handling of the SLAPP suit — particularly the contempt order prior to UPEPA adjudication — followed proper procedure.
