You Can Do This

Everything documented on this site was built from public records, court filings, FOIA responses, campaign finance data, and published reporting. None of it required a law license, a press badge, or a government position. It required attention, persistence, and a willingness to read more than one file at a time.

This section gives you the tools to do the same.

The Process

This is the process used to build detroit.primals.eco and this site:

  1. Find a thread — a name, a case number, a campaign contribution, a court filing that doesn't match what was said publicly
  2. Pull the records — FOIA the agency, search the docket, check LARA, run the campaign finance
  3. Read them against each other — a single record tells one story; three records from three systems tell the real one
  4. Document what you find — contemporaneous notes, screenshots with timestamps, original documents preserved
  5. Publish the sourced version — every claim tied to a specific document, docket entry, or filing
  6. File the complaints — AGC for attorneys, JTC for judges, SCAO for systemic court issues, MCOLES for law enforcement, IC3 for federal crimes
  7. Follow through — track responses, appeal denials, escalate when ignored

Why This Works

Michigan's oversight system dismisses 94% of complaints. Not because the complaints are unfounded — because the system is built to process them one at a time, in isolation, without pattern recognition.

A single FOIA denial is an inconvenience. Documented FOIA denials across three cases from the same prosecutor's office is a pattern. A pattern sourced to primary records is evidence. Evidence published where investigators can find it is pressure.

That is what this section is for.
