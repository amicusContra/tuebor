Evidence Documentation

Why Documentation Matters

Most people who are dealing with institutional misconduct lose the record before they know they need it. By the time they realize they need a timeline, the emails are deleted, the screenshots are gone, and memory has compressed months of events into "it happened around that time."

This guide gives you the framework to build a record that holds up.

Seven Rules for a Record That Holds Up

1. Write it down the same day

A note written the day it happened is a contemporaneous record. A note written three months later is a recollection. Courts and investigators treat them very differently.

2. Date and timestamp everything

Every entry: full date, time if you know it, and what happened. Not "last week" — "Tuesday, September 24, 2026, approximately 2:15 PM."

3. Facts, not feelings

Write what happened, what was said, who was present. Save your interpretation for a separate section. An investigator needs to see what occurred before they can evaluate what it means.

❌ Opinion✅ Fact
"The judge was biased against me""On [date], Judge [Name] stated on the record: '[exact quote]'"
"The prosecutor lied""On [date], APA [Name] stated in court that [X]. Court record [case number, docket entry] shows [Y], which contradicts that statement."
"They're retaliating""On [date], [event]. This occurred [X] days after I filed [complaint/FOIA/motion] on [date]."

4. Preserve originals

  • Emails: Forward to a separate email account you control. Print to PDF with full headers.
  • Web pages: Use archive.org Wayback Machine Save Page or screenshot with URL bar visible.
  • Court filings: Download PDFs from the docket. If paper only, photograph every page.
  • Text messages: Screenshot with date/time visible and the contact name/number at the top.
  • Social media: Screenshot entire post including URL, timestamp, and commenter identity.
  • Voicemail: Save the audio file. Note date, time, caller, and transcribe the content.

5. Use a document index

Number every document. Create a master list:

7. Redundancy

Store your records in at least two places. A USB drive in a drawer does not help if your laptop is stolen. Cloud backup (encrypted), a trusted friend or attorney, and a physical copy.

The Preservation Notice

When you believe records are at risk of destruction, send a written Preservation Notice (also called a "litigation hold" or "preservation demand"). This puts the institution on notice that they must preserve all relevant documents and electronically stored information.

Template

[Your Full Name]
[Your Mailing Address]
[Date]

[Institution Name]
[Address]
Attn: [FOIA Coordinator / Board Chair / Administrator]

RE: PRESERVATION NOTICE — DEMAND TO PRESERVE RECORDS

Dear [Name]:

This letter constitutes formal notice to preserve all documents,
records, communications, electronically stored information (ESI),
audio/video recordings, and tangible evidence related to:

[SPECIFIC DESCRIPTION — case numbers, dates, individuals, events]

This preservation obligation includes but is not limited to:
- Email communications (including deleted items and archives)
- Text messages and instant messages
- Voicemail recordings
- Meeting minutes and agendas
- Personnel files and internal memoranda
- Body camera, dashboard camera, and surveillance footage
- Financial records, invoices, and payment authorizations
- FOIA request logs and response files

Destruction, alteration, or concealment of these records after
receipt of this notice may constitute spoliation of evidence and
subject [Institution] to adverse inferences, sanctions, and
potential criminal liability.

This notice is effective immediately upon receipt and remains in
effect until further written notice.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]
[Your Printed Name]

Send via certified mail with return receipt (USPS Form 3800). Keep the tracking receipt.

Common Mistakes

MistakeWhy It Hurts
Waiting to documentMemory degrades. A 6-month-old recollection carries less weight than a same-day note.
Mixing facts with interpretationMakes the whole document easier to dismiss. Keep them separate.
Not preserving originalsA screenshot of an email is weaker than the email with full headers.
Filing without documentation"The judge was unfair" is an opinion. "On [date], Judge [Name] sentenced defendant to 36-60 months on 0-6 month guidelines (see D-015)" is evidence.
Keeping only one copyHard drives fail. Phones get stolen. Physical paper gets wet.

For Attorneys and Oversight Bodies

If you are an attorney, investigator, or oversight body reviewing documentation assembled using this framework:

  • Document Index entries reference specific exhibits by number
  • Escalation Tracker entries show procedural exhaustion and timeline
  • Preservation Notices establish the date of institutional notice
  • All factual claims should be verifiable against the cited source documents

The goal is to give you a record you can use without having to reconstruct it.
