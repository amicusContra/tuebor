## The 990-EZ

Every tax-exempt organization files a return with the IRS. Form 990 for the large ones. Form 990-EZ for the small. The filer enters its name, its mission, its revenue, its expenses.

And on page 4, under **Part IV — "List of Officers, Directors, Trustees, and Key Employees"** — it names the person who prepared the form.

For **Save Detroit Jobs**, tax year 2023, that person is **Olivia RC A Flower**.

Flower works at Dykema Gossett's **Ann Arbor** office.

Save Detroit Jobs was incorporated by **W. Alan Wilk**, who works at Dykema Gossett's **Lansing** office.

Save Detroit Jobs is governed by **Renae Moore**, who works at Dykema Gossett's **Lansing** office.

Three employees. Two offices. One vehicle.

## Why Ann Arbor?

Dykema Gossett operates from five Michigan offices: Detroit, Lansing, Ann Arbor, Grand Rapids, and Bloomfield Hills. The political compliance team — Wilk's team — is headquartered in Lansing. Phone extensions 9121 and 9122, one digit apart.

If the formation is in Lansing, and the governance is in Lansing, why is the tax filing in Ann Arbor?

The answer is on Dykema's own website. Their Ann Arbor office lists among its practice areas: **Tax.** The firm routes its tax compliance work to Ann Arbor. This is normal law firm architecture — specialized teams handle specialized work regardless of which office originated the client.

But it means the paperwork trail crosses offices. The formation attorney in Lansing hands the vehicle to the tax specialist in Ann Arbor, who files the 990-EZ with the IRS, which becomes a public record, which names the preparer.

The 990-EZ is the leak. The preparer field is the drip. One name — **Olivia RC A Flower** — connects two offices that were never supposed to be connected on paper.

The same law firm that **authored Michigan's charter school legislation** in the 1990s now forms the dark money vehicles that influence who sits on charter school boards. And the tax filings for those vehicles are prepared by a third employee at a third office.

The connection runs from Ann Arbor (tax filing) to Lansing (formation and governance) to Detroit (the council seat, the charter school, the nine-conviction superintendent).

## The Agent Selection

In the first seven vehicles, Wilk used **Corporation Company** as the registered agent — a professional service that provides a buffer between the incorporator and the public filing.

For vehicle #10 — **Michiganders for Progressive Values Fund**, incorporated September 2024 — Wilk named **himself** as registered agent. No Corporation Company. No buffer. No membrane between the attorney and the entity.

Why drop the buffer for this one?

We don't know. But the pattern shift is structural. It suggests either increased urgency (no time for Corporation Company setup), increased confidence (no perceived risk), or a different purpose for this vehicle that doesn't require the same distance.

The agent selection is itself a data point. When the architect removes his own buffer, the architecture is speaking.

## The Leak

In plumbing, a leak doesn't just release water. It reveals pressure. Something inside the system is pushing outward against the seams. The water finds the weakest joint. The drip tells you which pipe is under stress.

The 990-EZ naming Olivia Flower is a drip. It tells you the cross-office pipe is under pressure — that the Lansing formation machine needs Ann Arbor's tax infrastructure to complete the circuit. The drip wasn't supposed to be visible. Tax preparers on 990-EZ filings are public record, but no one was supposed to look.

Someone looked.

The flower didn't want to wilk. But the filing is public. The connection is made. And the dyke — the wall between the formation office and the tax office, between the political compliance team and the financial compliance team, between Lansing and Ann Arbor — has one more crack.
