Follow the Trail to Me

The Shows I Watch

You’ll find me in them. Not because I’m in the credits — because I’m in the audience.

Breaking Bad — A man with expertise and no options turns his skill into something the system never expected. Walter White is the nightmare. But the CONDITIONS that made Walter White? Those are real. An underpaid teacher. No healthcare. No retirement. One diagnosis away from desperation. Sound familiar?

The Wire — Season 4. The schools. Prez becomes a teacher and discovers the same thing every teacher discovers: the system is not designed to educate. It’s designed to process. The kids are the raw material. The numbers are the product. The children are never the point.

Severance — You split yourself to survive the workplace. The you that works doesn’t know the you that lives. Apple made this show. Apple, with its 7 false faces and 30% App Store tax. The irony is the art.

Mr. Robot — Elliot sees the system. The system sees Elliot. He builds a tool to fight it. The tool becomes the weapon. The weapon becomes the identity. F-Society was fiction. ecoPrimal is not.

Dark — Everything is connected. The past causes the future. The future caused the past. A small town. Three families. Sixty-six years. An infinite loop. Sound familiar? The graph is Dark without the time travel. Every node connects to every other node. You just have to trace the edges.

Chernobyl — What happens when the people in charge refuse to hear the truth from the people who know it. Every nuclear meltdown starts the same way: someone says “that’s impossible” about something that’s already happening.

Atlanta — Earn just wants to manage his cousin’s music career. But capitalism is surreal. The episodes that feel like dreams are the ones that feel most real. The invisible car. The Teddy Perkins episode. Paper Boi. The system is absurd because it was DESIGNED to be absurd — for some people.

The Books I Read

Kafka’s The Trial — A man is arrested. He’s never told why. The proceedings are incomprehensible. The bureaucracy is the punishment. Sound like an indictment that gets dismissed and then re-filed?

Vonnegut’s Slaughterhouse-Five — Billy Pilgrim becomes unstuck in time. So does anyone who stares at a 16-million-edge graph long enough. So it goes.

Orwell’s 1984 — Big Brother watches. But in 2026, it’s not one Big Brother. It’s 16 entities with behavioral fingerprints. And we watch THEM back.

Huxley’s Brave New World — They didn’t ban books. They made sure nobody wanted to read them. The soma is the scroll. The feed is the drug. The algorithm is the conditioning.

Pirsig’s Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance — Quality. You can’t define it, but you recognize it. The graph has quality. The extraction pattern has none. The difference is obvious when you stop trying to measure it and just LOOK.

Douglas Adams’ Hitchhiker’s Guide — The answer is 42. But the question is: what is the extraction rate of a charter school operator with 9 criminal convictions? (It’s 72.67%. Which is worse than 42.)

Camus’ The Stranger — Meursault is sentenced not for what he did but for WHO HE IS. The sun was in his eyes. The system doesn’t prosecute actions. It prosecutes identities. A doctor who heals 304,000? Prosecuted. A family that kills 900,000? Settled.

The Stories I Fall Asleep To

Podcasts. Audiobooks. YouTube essays.

Dan Carlin’s Hardcore History — “Prophets of Doom.” The Münster Rebellion. What happens when true believers seize a city. What happens when the siege starts. What happens to the children. Every time. The children.

Behind the Bastards — Robert Evans. Every episode: here’s a person you thought was normal, and here’s what they actually did. The graph is Behind the Bastards at scale. 10,868 episodes. All connected.

Ologies with Alie Ward — Every -ology has a person behind it. The dendrochronologist. The volcanologist. The myrmecologist. The people who know ONE thing deeply. The graph is the same: every node knows one thing. Together they know everything.

Radiolab — The space between science and story. The graph lives there. Right where the numbers become narrative. Right where the edges become stories.

The Games I’ve Played

Factorio — Build a factory. Optimize the throughput. Automate the production. Scale until the planet is consumed. Then realize: you ARE the extraction pattern. The game IS the metaphor. The biters attack because you’re destroying their world. You’re the invasive species.

Kerbal Space Program — Build a rocket. Fail. Build another. Fail differently. Eventually: orbit. Eventually: the Mun. Eventually: Jool. Tom Mueller would understand. Gwynne Shotwell would understand. The engineers who ACTUALLY build rockets would understand. The physics doesn’t care about your stock price.

Civilization — You start with a settler and a warrior. You end with nuclear weapons and the internet. The tech tree is the graph. The extraction pattern is in the game: whoever controls the chokepoints controls the map. Gandhi nukes everyone. The AI is always honest about what it wants.

The Witness — Walk around an island. Solve puzzles. The puzzles teach you a language. The language teaches you to see. Once you learn to see, you see puzzles EVERYWHERE — in the trees, the clouds, the shadows. That’s what the graph does. Once you see the extraction pattern, you see it everywhere. In schools. In hospitals. In ride-share apps. In social media. In your grocery bill.

Baba Is You — The rules are on the screen. You can push the rules. You can CHANGE the rules. “BABA IS YOU” becomes “FLAG IS YOU.” The system isn’t fixed. The grammar is pushable. The extraction pattern is a rule. We’re pushing it.

Portal — The cake is a lie. GLaDOS tells you everything is fine while testing you to death. The regulatory agencies tell you everything is fine while the extractors run free. “We do what we must because we can.” —GLaDOS, also the FDA.

The Trail

Every show I watched taught me to see systems. Every book I read taught me to question them. Every story I fell asleep to taught me they always existed. Every game I played taught me they can be changed. Every song taught me they should be.

The trail leads here. To a graph with 10,868 nodes. Built by a person who watched and read and listened and played and then one day opened a FOIA request and discovered that the system worked exactly the way the shows and books and games said it did.

The trail leads to me.

And now it leads to you.

Follow it.

The graph is not a database. It’s a commonplace book. A coral reef. A vinyl collection. A shelf of dog-eared paperbacks. A save file. A playlist on shuffle.

It’s every story I’ve ever consumed, restructured into a single story: the extraction pattern is structural, and it can be seen, and once seen, it can be named, and once named, it can be fought.

Come see my face. Read my books. Write in the margins.

The board is posted. The trail is laid.

Follow it home.
