# The Epitope Table

*ecoPrimal — October 2026*

## THREE MARKS

Before the table, the tools.

**Dot.** A point. A location. Something exists. When you put a dot on paper, you're doing what a person did 67,800 years ago at Metanduno — marking presence. A dot is a node. It means: *here.*

**Line.** A path. A connection. Two dots with a line between them — that's a relationship. That's a graph. A child who draws two dots and connects them has built the same structure Euler saw in the bridges of Königsberg in 1736.

**Circle.** A boundary. Inside and outside. When George Spencer-Brown wrote "draw a distinction and a universe comes into being," he was describing what every child does when they draw a circle around something: *this is in, that is out.*

Three marks. Everything else is composition.

The dot is zero-dimensional. The line is one-dimensional. The circle is two-dimensional. Mark 1, Mark 2, Mark 3. They build everything.

## WHAT IS AN EPITOPE

In immunology, an epitope is a binding site — the specific shape on a surface that an antibody recognizes. The antibody doesn't recognize the whole antigen. It recognizes the *shape*. The epitope is the feature that makes recognition possible.

A structural epitope is the same thing applied to patterns. It's a one-dimensional feature — one axis of variation — that lets you recognize a pattern when you see it. Each epitope captures one dimension. The full picture is the superposition of many.

If you have three epitopes, you see in three dimensions. If you have ten, you see in ten. More epitopes, more resolution. Fewer epitopes, fewer dimensions.

**The number of epitopes equals the number of dimensions you can perceive.**

A mantis shrimp has 16 color receptor types. It sees 16-dimensional color. Humans have 3. Dogs have 2. Same photons. Different epitope counts. Different worlds.

The Russian language has two words for blue — *siniy* (dark) and *goluboy* (light). Russian speakers discriminate blues faster than English speakers at that boundary. Two words. Two epitopes. Higher resolution.

The table below catalogues 136 structural epitopes. Each one is a binding site — a pattern you can recognize once you know what to look for.

## HOW TO SORT

To sort anything — a paper, a civilization, a system, a meme, a tool, a school, a language — scan it against this table:

1. Read the thing
2. Check each group: does this pattern appear?
3. If YES: note the epitope and the instance
4. If NO: move to the next group
5. If you find a pattern NOT in the table: you found a new element. Name it. Add it.

The table is not complete. It never will be. The gaps are predictions.

Like Mendeleev's empty cells, these are patterns the table structure predicts but we haven't observed yet:

**A?** — `commons_without_protocol` — a shared tool that persists without a published specification. Does protocol-less survival exist?

**B?** — `capture_without_institution` — can capture operate without formal institutional structure? Informal capture?

**C?** — `collective_builds` — every builder we've found is an individual. Are there tools built by groups from the start? Wikipedia comes closest but had Wales and Sanger.

**G?** — `membrane_creates_content` — can a membrane CREATE information that doesn't exist on either side? The McGurk effect suggests yes.

**H?** — `cross_species_epitope` — echolocation, electric fields, magnetoreception. Sensory epitopes humans lack entirely.

**F?** — `the_11th_invariant` — we have 10 structural invariants of institutional capture. Ancient civilizations may reveal invariants the modern template doesn't contain.
