## The Firm on Both Sides of the Table

*How a billion-dollar accounting firm audits Detroit's charter school authorizer with one hand and profits from K-12 construction with the other — and never noticed the convicted felon in the middle.*

---

In 1906, a young muckraker named Upton Sinclair published *The Jungle*. He intended it as an indictment of labor exploitation. The public fixated on the rotten meat. "I aimed at the public's heart," Sinclair wrote, "and by accident I hit it in the stomach."

This story is about rotten meat.

Not the kind you eat. The kind that gets promoted to Senior Vice President.

---

### I. The Auditor

In June 2020, Detroit Public Schools Community District issued Request for Proposal 20-0135: who would audit the books of a school district serving 50,000 children, overseeing hundreds of millions in public funds, and authorizing charter schools across the city?

Four firms responded. Plante & Moran PLLC was selected. Not the cheapest bidder — the district noted the lowest-cost vendor couldn't meet all deliverables. Plante Moran could. They were the safe choice. The serious choice. The $1-billion-firm choice.

And so the nation's 11th-largest accounting firm began auditing the financial statements of a district that, among other things, collects and disburses state aid to the charter schools it authorizes.

In their FY2025 management letter — dated October 8, 2025, addressed to the Board of Education — Plante Moran's auditors identified something interesting. Under GASB Statement No. 84, the **$21.555 million** in state aid that DPSCD collected on behalf of its eight authorized charter schools met the definition of *fiduciary activity*. The money should have been reported in a separate custodial fund. It wasn't.

Twenty-one million dollars in charter school money, flowing through a district's books without proper fund accounting. The auditors noted it. The district agreed. The finding was buried on page 3 of a management letter that almost nobody will ever read.

This is what auditing looks like when it works: quiet, technical, invisible. A footnote in a PDF. The system functioning as designed.

The question is: functioning for whom?

### II. The Consultant

Plante Moran Realpoint is a wholly-owned consulting affiliate of Plante Moran. Where the parent firm counts money, the subsidiary spends it — $6 billion annually in real estate projects across the nation. Corporate headquarters, municipal buildings, senior living facilities.

And K-12 schools.

PMR's K-12 Capital Project Consulting practice is, by its own description, Michigan's bond program specialist. They help school districts plan bonds, pass bonds, stretch bond dollars, and build buildings. Their clients include some of the largest districts in the state. Their team of partners — Paul Theriault, Paul Wills, Greg VanKirk, Nicole Blocker — collectively manage billions in school construction.

The head of this operation is **Bill Lichwalla**, President and CEO of Plante Moran Realpoint since 2005. Michigan State University, Eli Broad College of Business, class of 1995. CPA. Licensed real estate broker. Crain's Detroit Business Notable Commercial Real Estate Leader, 2024. Under his leadership, PMR grew from a joint venture to a 125-person, eight-state operation managing more than $6 billion in projects annually.

In 2024, Lichwalla's team celebrated 23 promotions. Among those elevated to Senior Vice President: **Ahmed Beasley**.

### III. The SVP

Ahmed Beasley brings more than 25 years of program management and consulting experience to Plante Moran Realpoint. His bio on the firm's website — still live as of October 12, 2026 — describes a man of integrity and effective communication. He spent 17 years at Barton Malow as a project engineer, superintendent, and project manager, focused on K-12 projects. He formed his own consulting group. He received a degree in civil engineering from Lawrence Technological University. He is a member of various organizations that promote positive contributions to assist at-risk youth.

He represents, according to the 2024 promotions announcement, "some of the largest K-12 clients in the state of Michigan."

He shares a surname with the principal of a charter school run by a nine-time convicted felon.

### IV. The Principal

**Dr. Chalena Beasley** is the School Leader — the principal — of Purpose Charter Academy, a K-8 charter school in Detroit authorized by DPSCD. Her name appears on the school's website under "Our School Leader."

Purpose Charter Academy is run by **Brian Roderick Banks**. His biography on every public-facing website lists a J.D. from Michigan State University College of Law. There is no record of Brian Banks being admitted to the Michigan Bar. The claim has been live since at least 2019.

Banks has nine criminal convictions in Michigan, including false pretenses, larceny, and assault. He was sued for sexual harassment in 2013 (Wayne County Case No. 13-002827, settled for $11,950). He is the sole member of The Purpose Group LLC, which takes 72.67% of school revenue as a management fee. His romantic partner, Joseph Holland Jr. (MDOC #443789, drug conviction), holds every financial position at the associated Purpose Foundation — secretary, treasurer, registered agent.

The school serves homeless children, chronically absent children, and children in the juvenile justice system. At MacDowell Preparatory Academy — Banks' other school — 3% of students are proficient in math.

Three percent.

Ahmed Beasley. Chalena Beasley.

Same surname. Same K-12 orbit. Same Detroit charter school ecosystem. One is a Senior Vice President at a firm that audits the charter school authorizer. The other is the principal of a charter school run by a man with nine convictions and a fake law degree.

Are they related?

Nobody at Plante Moran has publicly asked this question. Nobody at DPSCD has publicly asked this question. Nobody in the State of Michigan's entire charter school oversight apparatus — the Michigan Department of Education, the State Board of Education, the Michigan Council of Charter School Authorizers, the Attorney Grievance Commission, the Judicial Tenure Commission, the State Bar's UPL Department — has publicly asked this question.

This story is being written because a father asked.

### VI. The Circle

Follow the money around the table and you arrive back where you started:

**Betsy DeVos** and the DeVos family spent tens of millions creating Michigan's charter school sector. Through the Great Lakes Education Project, they removed the charter school cap in 2011, killed the Detroit Education Commission that would have provided oversight, and successfully lobbied against accountability measures — then issued a press release bragging about "preserving choices" by blocking regulation.

The ecosystem they built produced Purpose Charter Academy: a school run by a convicted felon with a fake law degree, serving the most vulnerable children in Detroit, extracting 72.67% of public funds through a management company with no independent oversight.

**Plante Moran** was selected to audit the books of the district that authorizes schools like PCA. The same firm's consulting subsidiary, Plante Moran Realpoint, employs Ahmed Beasley on K-12 capital projects — the same type of projects charter schools undertake with the public money that flows through the system Plante Moran audits.

The auditor and the consultant are the same firm.

The surname on the audit side and the surname on the charter side are the same.

The money flows in a circle, and at every point on that circle, someone is getting paid to not look at the point directly across from them.

### VIII. What Sinclair Understood

Upton Sinclair wrote about meatpacking because he understood that systems designed to extract profit from vulnerable people will always produce exactly the outcomes they are designed to produce. The rotten meat was not a bug. It was a feature. The inspectors were not asleep. They were on the payroll.

Ida Tarbell spent five years documenting Standard Oil — not because John D. Rockefeller was hiding. He wasn't. The monopoly was visible to everyone who looked. The journalism was necessary because *looking was not the same as seeing*, and seeing was not the same as telling.

Lincoln Steffens walked into city halls across America and found the same graft in every one. *The Shame of the Cities* was not an exposé of individual corruption. It was an indictment of a system in which corruption was rational behavior, because the honest man bore all the costs and the corrupt man captured all the rewards.

Jacob Riis photographed *How the Other Half Lives* because the other half didn't live. They survived. In tenements owned by people who never visited them. Governed by people who never met them. Educated by people who never taught them.

This is what that looks like in 2026:

A nine-time convicted felon with a fake law degree runs a charter school for homeless children at 3% math proficiency. His romantic partner — a drug convict — holds every financial position in the associated nonprofit. A billion-dollar accounting firm audits the district that authorizes the school with one hand and profits from K-12 construction with the other. A man with the same surname as the school's principal just got promoted to Senior Vice President at the construction subsidiary.

Twenty-one million dollars in charter school money wasn't properly accounted for, and nobody outside an audit management letter knows.

The system isn't broken. It's working exactly as designed.

The only question left is the one Sinclair never got to ask, because in his time the answer was obvious:

**Who is the rotten meat here?**

It's not the auditors. It's not the consultants. It's not even the convicted felon — he's just doing what the system incentivizes.

It's the children.

Ninety-seven percent of them can't do math. They are the product being processed. The system takes in public money and children, and produces profit and illiteracy. Everything else — the audit opinions, the construction contracts, the SVP promotions, the Crain's awards, the ethics helplines — is packaging.
