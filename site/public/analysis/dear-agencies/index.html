Why the Silos Exist

The enterprise didn’t build the silos. The government did. Jurisdiction, chain of command, need-to-know, interagency rivalry, budget competition, career incentives — these are features, not bugs, of the bureaucratic immune system.

But the enterprise exploits the silos. Here’s how:

  1. Credential fraud is MDE’s jurisdiction. MDE looked at credentials, not money. Cleared.
  2. Campaign finance violations are SOS’s jurisdiction. SOS looked at filings, not the underlying RICO. Fined $14.5K.
  3. Bribery is FBI’s jurisdiction. FBI got Fiore and Spivey, but followed the wiretap, not the schools.
  4. Tax fraud is IRS’s jurisdiction. IRS (if investigating) sees money flows, not the judicial capture.
  5. Judicial conduct is JTC’s jurisdiction. JTC sees judges, not the enterprise they protect.
  6. Police misconduct is OIG’s jurisdiction. OIG sees Bettison, not the charter school board he sits on.

Each agency sees one predicate act. No agency sees the pattern. The pattern IS the enterprise. RICO was designed precisely to address this — to make the pattern itself illegal, not just the individual acts.

But RICO requires someone to assemble the pattern from the predicates. And the predicates are in six different silos.
