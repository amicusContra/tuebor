The Board That Bored — Michigan's State Board of Education and the Oversight That Overslept

October 14, 2026

The Board That Bored

The Michigan State Board of Education has 8 elected members.

They set education policy for 1.4 million students.

They have 2 edges in the investigation graph.

Two.

What the SBE Does (On Paper)

The State Board of Education is constitutionally empowered to:

  1. Lead and supervise public education in Michigan
  2. Advise the legislature on education policy
  3. Appoint the Superintendent of Public Instruction
  4. Set standards for schools, including charter schools
  5. Oversee authorizers who approve charter schools

This is the top of the pyramid. If MDE is the engine, SBE is the steering wheel.

The SBE connects to the investigation graph at exactly 2 points.

The Classification

SBE: partial, 0.80.

Partial. Not self. The membrane doesn't classify SBE as a functioning oversight body.

MDE (Michigan Department of Education): partial, 0.80. Also partial. The enforcement arm doesn't function either.

The whole oversight stack is partial:

  • SBE: partial (sets policy — doesn't enforce)
  • MDE: partial (enforces — doesn't act)
  • DPSCD: partial (authorizes — doesn't revoke)
  • CMU: partial (authorizes — doesn't check backgrounds)

Every layer of oversight is classified the same way: partial. Each one does part of its job and ignores the rest.

The result: a school with 3% math proficiency and 72.67% extraction, run by a man with 9 criminal convictions, authorized by multiple bodies, overseen by nobody.

The Authorizer Problem

The SBE's most critical failure is authorizer oversight.

In Michigan, charter schools are authorized by:

  • Universities (like CMU)
  • School districts (like DPSCD)
  • Community college districts
  • Intermediate school districts

The SBE sets the standards for authorizers. It decides what qualifications authorizers must meet. It decides how much accountability authorizers must demonstrate.

Here's what those standards allowed:

The SBE's authorizer standards do not require a criminal background check for charter school founders.

A substitute teacher needs a background check. A bus driver needs a background check. The founder of a school does not.

The SBE sets those standards. The SBE could change them tomorrow.

They haven't.

The Silence

2 edges.

In a graph with 131,329 edges, 2 is not a connection. It's a disconnection.

It means: the State Board of Education is so uninvolved in the investigation of charter school fraud in their own state that they barely register as existing.

They're not covering anything up. They're not enabling anything directly. They're just... not there.

And that absence is the gap. That's where Banks operates. In the space between the board and the boardroom. In the distance between policy and enforcement. In the boredom of a board that bored.
