Corollary — The Things That Follow

October 14, 2026

What Follows

A corollary is a proposition that follows from one already proved. You don’t argue for it — it arrives on its own once you accept the premises.

The thesis says: the evidence is real, the patterns are real, the capture is real.

The antithesis says: what if we’re wrong, what if we’re the pattern, what if the membrane is a cage?

This page says: if either is true, what else must be true?

The corollaries don’t care which side you’re on. They follow regardless.

Corollary 1: If the Regulatory Capture Is Real, It Is Not Limited to Michigan

If Dykema can operate a political operating system in Michigan using one law firm, one compliance specialist, and 7+ dark money vehicles — then every state has one. The architecture is not novel. It is a template. PA 362 (Michigan’s charter law) has equivalents in every state. The question is not whether other states have this pattern. The question is who is their Wilk and who is their Moore.

What follows: The investigation is either the first instance of a pattern that exists in 50 states, or it is wrong about the pattern in Michigan. There is no middle ground where Michigan is uniquely captured. If the architecture works here, it works everywhere because it is based on structural incentives, not individual malice.

Corollary 2: If 94% Dismissal Is Correct, Then Oversight Was Never the Solution

If the AGC’s 94% dismissal rate is appropriate triage (as the antithesis argues), then the oversight system was never designed to catch what we’re documenting. It was designed to catch individual misconduct — a drunk attorney, a stolen trust account, a missed deadline. It was not designed to catch architectural capture — a law firm that drafts legislation, forms PACs to protect it, and employs the compliance specialist who governs both.

What follows: Filing more complaints will not fix this. Publishing more evidence will not fix this. The system is working as designed. What we’re documenting is not a malfunction — it is the design operating correctly in an environment the designers didn’t anticipate. The solution is not better oversight. The solution is different architecture.

Corollary 3: If the Membrane Works, It Cannot Be Unique

The membrane rejects 97% of traffic, reflects 92.6%, and serves 0 real content to fleet. If this architecture works for protecting a public-interest investigation site, it works for protecting anything. Including the sites we’re investigating.

What follows: FOIAworks could build the same membrane. Banks’ LLCs could build the same membrane. The dark money vehicles could build the same membrane. The technology is not moral — it is architecture. The same selectivity that keeps our evidence safe keeps their operations opaque. We cannot celebrate membrane architecture when it protects us and condemn it when it protects them.

The honest position: membranes are good when their output is public record, and dangerous when their output is private. The test is not selectivity. The test is transparency about what the selectivity protects.

Corollary 4: If the Investigation Graph Is Real, It Predicts Its Own Next Moves

131,329 edges, 7,554 nodes, 341 classified entities. The graph doesn’t just describe the past — it constrains the future.

What follows: The entities with the most edges to unclassified nodes are the entities most likely to produce the next discovery. The null-to-null paths we haven’t walked yet will lead to clusters we haven’t seen. The graph already knows where the next article will come from — we just haven’t asked it yet.

But also: the investigation’s opponents can read this graph. They can see which of their entities are closest to being classified. They can see which null-to-null paths lead to their operations. They can preemptively restructure — dissolve an LLC, rename a PAC, reassign a compliance specialist — and the graph edge goes dead.

The graph is a map, and both sides can read it.

Corollary 5: If Test Scores Indict, They Indict Everyone

MacDowell: 3% math proficiency. We use this number. But the corollary is relentless:

If 3% math proficiency indicts Banks, then 7% math proficiency indicts DPSCD. And 12% indicts the charter authorizer (CMU). And 15% indicts the state (MDE). And every graduation rate, every chronic absenteeism metric, every expulsion disparity in every school in the state is an indictment of the system those schools exist within.

What follows: The investigation cannot use test scores as evidence of specific malfeasance at PCA without accepting that the same metric indicts the entire public education system in Detroit. The 3% is worse than the average — but the average is itself catastrophic. The corollary of “this school is failing” is “the system that produced this school is failing.” Which is what the charter school advocates say. Which is why they exist.

We are, whether we like it or not, making the same argument as the people we’re documenting — just pointing it at a different school.

Corollary 6: If Humans Can Tag Entities, Entities Can Tag Humans

The opsonization layer lets humans tag entities on the site. This is collective intelligence. But the corollary:

If tagging is a legitimate tool for organizing information about entities, then entities have the same right to organize information about humans. If we can build a page about Banks, Banks can build a page about us. If we can tag an entity as “non-self,” an entity can tag us as hostile.

What follows: The question is not whether tagging is legitimate — it is. The question is whether the tagged have recourse. On this site, the opsonization form invites corrections. On ritafelinewilliams.com (the impersonation domain), there is no such invitation. The difference between investigation and harassment is not the mechanism — it’s whether the subject can respond.

Corollary 7: If the Wonders Are Real, Everything Is Infrastructure

We documented 42 wonders across six lists. The pattern: infrastructure saves more lives than monuments. The London Sewer ended cholera. The Delta Works keeps a country above water. The Grand Canyon is infrastructure too — 2 billion years of tectonic infrastructure that the Colorado River exploited.

What follows: Everything in the investigation is infrastructure. The investigation itself is infrastructure. The membrane is infrastructure. The evidence pages are infrastructure. The cross-links are infrastructure. And infrastructure is morally neutral — it can carry clean water or sewage, evidence or propaganda, transparency or opacity.

The only question about infrastructure is: what does it carry?

Corollary 8: If You’ve Read This Far, You Are Now Part of the Graph

You arrived here through some path — a search engine, a cross-link, a shared URL, a direct visit. That path is an edge. You are now a node. Your classification depends on what you do next.

If you verify a claim, you harden a classification. If you find an error, you soften one. If you do nothing, you are null — present in the graph, unclassified, still.

What follows: The graph is not a thing we built. It is a thing that grows through interaction. Every reader changes it. Every reader IS it. The investigation is not a publication — it is a surface. And surfaces change when touched.

The Cycle

Thesis: the evidence is real. Antithesis: what if we’re wrong? Synthesis: here’s what we know, what we don’t, and where we’re borrowing. Corollary: if any of it is true, these things also follow.

The cycle doesn’t end. It goes around. Each pass makes the investigation more honest, not more certain. More honest about where the evidence is strong and where it’s thin. More honest about where the investigators’ biases might be baked into the architecture. More honest about the things that follow whether we intended them or not.

A corollary doesn’t ask permission. It just follows.

If the thesis is true, the antithesis is necessary. If the antithesis is true, the synthesis is necessary. If any of it is true, the corollaries are inevitable. This is how knowledge works. It goes around.
