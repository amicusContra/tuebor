## The Testimony

On February 26, 2025, Renae Moore took the witness stand in Ingham County District Court. She was the first witness called in the preliminary examination of former Michigan House Speaker Lee Chatfield, who faces 13 felony charges.

Moore is a Senior Compliance Specialist at Dykema Gossett PLLC, Michigan's largest law firm.

She testified that she serves as Assistant Secretary/Treasurer of the Peninsula Fund, Chatfield's 501(c)(4) political nonprofit that raised $1.8 million from anonymous donors.

She testified that she manages **more than 80 similar accounts** at Dykema.

She testified that she flagged **"questionable" expenses**, including a **strip club tab** submitted for reimbursement.

She testified that the fund was sometimes **"not compliant"** with the law.

And when Judge Molly Hennessey Greenwalt asked her directly whether she had a duty to report concerns about potentially illegal transactions to anyone, Moore replied:

> **"No. Not that I am aware of."**

Moore's Law: *I have no duty to report.*

**Moore's Law is false.**

## Proof #1: Michigan Nonprofit Corporation Act

**MCL 450.2541(1):**

> *"A director or officer shall discharge his or her duties as a director or officer... in good faith and with the degree of diligence, care, and skill that an ordinarily prudent person would exercise under similar circumstances in a like position."*

Moore was an **officer** of the Peninsula Fund. Assistant Secretary/Treasurer is a statutory officer position under Michigan's Nonprofit Corporation Act.

Officers owe a **duty of care**: act with diligence. A **duty of loyalty**: act in the organization's interest. A **duty of good faith**: don't ignore what you see.

And the statute has a trap door — **MCL 450.2541(3)**:

> *"A director or officer is NOT entitled to rely on [reports from others] if he or she has knowledge concerning the matter in question that makes reliance otherwise permitted... UNWARRANTED."*

Moore had knowledge. She flagged the strip club tab. She identified questionable expenses. She testified the fund was sometimes not compliant. **Once you know the spending is questionable, you cannot rely on being told it's fine.** The statute says so explicitly.

The Clark Hill Nonprofit Guide for Michigan directors:

> *"The 'honorary' officer should take seriously the responsibility of monitoring the performance of the individuals to whom these functions have been delegated."*

Moore was not honorary. She was the **Senior Compliance Specialist.**

| Element | What the Law Requires | What Moore Did |
|---------|----------------------|----------------|
| Good faith | Act honestly | Flagged problems, then accepted "it was permitted" |
| Diligence | Investigate concerns | Testified she relied on Anne Minard's determinations |
| Care | Ordinarily prudent person standard | An ordinarily prudent compliance specialist reports strip club tabs |
| Knowledge exception | Cannot rely on others once you KNOW | She KNEW. She flagged it. Reliance became "unwarranted." |

**Verdict: Moore had a statutory duty under MCL 450.2541. She said she didn't. Moore's Law is false.**

## Proof #2: Federal Election Campaign Act

Renae Moore is not just an assistant treasurer of nonprofits. She is the **TREASURER** of the Dykema Gossett Federal PAC (FEC ID: C00342113).

**11 CFR 104.14(d):**

> *"Each treasurer of a political committee... shall be personally responsible for the timely and complete filing of the report or statement and for the accuracy of any information or statement contained in it."*

The FEC's Policy Statement on Treasurer's Liability (70 Fed. Reg. 3, January 2005):

> *"Due to their 'pivotal role,' treasurers may be held personally liable for failing to fulfill their responsibilities under the Act."*

Personal liability attaches when a treasurer:

1. **Knowingly and willfully** violates the Act
2. **Recklessly fails** to fulfill duties specifically imposed by the Act
3. **Intentionally deprives** himself or herself of facts giving rise to the violation

Moore managed 80+ accounts. She saw questionable spending across multiple funds. She was told it was permitted and accepted that answer without independent verification. **That is textbook "intentionally depriving herself of the operative facts."**

The same person who said she had "no duty to report" is the **treasurer of a federal PAC that funded 47 candidates with $131,000** in the current cycle. The FEC says she is **personally responsible** for the accuracy of every filing.

| FEC Requirement | Moore's Role | The Problem |
|----------------|-------------|-------------|
| Personally responsible for accuracy | TREASURER of C00342113 | Same person managing nonprofit funds with "questionable" spending |
| Must examine contributions for illegality | Duty under 11 CFR 103.3 | Testified she accepted spending as "permitted" without verification |
| Preserve records for 3 years | Duty under 52 U.S.C. § 30102 | Records from Peninsula Fund are central to Chatfield prosecution |

**Verdict: Moore is personally liable as FEC treasurer under 11 CFR 104.14(d). She said she had no duty. Moore's Law is false.**

## Proof #3: Internal Revenue Code — 501(c)(4) Inurement

**IRC § 501(c)(4)** expressly prohibits the inurement of net earnings to the benefit of any private individual.

Chatfield allegedly used Peninsula Fund money for:
- Personal credit card payments ($132,000+ over 15 months)
- Family vacation at Universal Studios in Orlando
- A strip club tab
- Checks to family members (kickback scheme)

That is **inurement**. That is an **excess benefit transaction** under IRC § 4958.

The IRS requires Form 990 filers to disclose on **Part VI, Section B, Line 5** whether the organization became aware of a **material diversion of its assets**.

Moore was the compliance officer who prepared or oversaw the 990 filings. She identified questionable spending. She testified the fund was not always compliant. If the 990s were filed without disclosing known diversions, those filings are **incomplete or inaccurate.**

Penalties for incomplete/inaccurate 990 filings:
- **$25/day** against the organization (up to $13,000 or 5% of gross receipts)
- **$10/day** against the responsible person after IRS demand (up to $6,500)
- **Criminal penalties** (IRC §§ 7203, 7206, 7207) for willfully filing fraudulent returns

Officers who participate in excess benefit transactions face excise taxes:
- **25% of the excess benefit** on the disqualified person
- **10% on each manager who participated** (up to $20,000 per transaction)

A "manager who participated" includes any officer who **knew** about the transaction and did not attempt to correct it.

Moore knew. She flagged it. She didn't correct it. She filed (or helped file) the returns anyway.

**Verdict: Moore had IRS-imposed duties as an officer of a 501(c)(4). She said she had none. Moore's Law is false.**

## Proof #4: Common Law Fiduciary Duty

Even without statutes, Moore owed a **common law fiduciary duty** as an officer of the Peninsula Fund.

Three prongs:

1. **Duty of Care**: Act as a reasonably prudent person would in similar circumstances. A reasonably prudent compliance specialist reports strip club tabs submitted as business expenses for a social welfare nonprofit.

2. **Duty of Loyalty**: Act in the organization's best interest, not in the interest of any individual (including the firm that pays your salary). Moore's loyalty to Dykema's client relationship cannot override her duty to the Peninsula Fund.

3. **Duty of Obedience**: Ensure the organization operates according to its stated mission. The Peninsula Fund's mission: *"promote civic action and social welfare."* Strip club tabs and personal credit cards do not promote civic action or social welfare. **Moore wrote the policy manual that said so.** Then she watched the policy get violated.

She wrote the rules. She watched them break. She said she had no duty to say anything.

**Verdict: Common law imposes fiduciary duties on nonprofit officers. Moore said she had no duty. Moore's Law is false.**
