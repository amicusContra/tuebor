## Scan, Sort, Wonder

We scanned the [state lines and mountains](/evidence/every-line-every-peak-every-depth/). We scanned the [towers and satellites](/evidence/every-tower-every-wire-every-orbit/). Now we scan the things humans decided were worth pointing at and saying: *look at this.*

There is no single official list. There are at least **six** major "Seven Wonders" lists, compiled by different people across 2,200 years for different reasons. Plus 1,273 UNESCO World Heritage Sites. We sort them all.

---

## The Seven Wonders of the Ancient World

Compiled by Philo of Byzantium, ~225 BC. Seven structures a Greek traveler should see. All were within the Hellenistic world — the Mediterranean and Near East. **Only one survives.**

| # | Wonder | Location | Built | Height/Size | Status |
|---|--------|----------|-------|-------------|--------|
| 1 | **Great Pyramid of Giza** | Giza, Egypt | ~2560 BC | 146 m (481 ft) original; 230 m base | **STILL STANDING** |
| 2 | Hanging Gardens of Babylon | Hillah or Nineveh, Iraq | ~600 BC | Unknown | **Existence disputed. No trace.** |
| 3 | Statue of Zeus at Olympia | Olympia, Greece | 435 BC | 12 m (40 ft) tall | Destroyed by fire, 5th–6th c. AD |
| 4 | Temple of Artemis | Selçuk, Turkey | ~550 BC | 129 × 69 m footprint | Burned 356 BC; rebuilt; sacked by Goths 262 AD. Column fragments remain. |
| 5 | Mausoleum at Halicarnassus | Bodrum, Turkey | 351 BC | ~45 m (148 ft) tall | Earthquakes, 12th–15th c. Fragments at British Museum. |
| 6 | Colossus of Rhodes | Rhodes, Greece | 280 BC | ~33 m (108 ft) tall | Earthquake, 226 BC. No trace. |
| 7 | Lighthouse of Alexandria | Alexandria, Egypt | ~280 BC | 110–134 m (360–440 ft) | Earthquakes, 956–1480 AD. Underwater remains found 1994. |

### The Sort

All seven were within 2,500 km of each other. Two in Egypt, two in Greece, two in Turkey, one in Iraq. The list was a Greek tourist guide — it mapped the wonders you could reach by boat from the eastern Mediterranean.

The Great Pyramid is the oldest and the only survivor. It was already **2,200 years old** when Philo compiled his list. It is now **4,584 years old**. It was the tallest human-made structure on Earth for 3,800 years (until Lincoln Cathedral, ~1300 AD). 2.3 million limestone blocks. Average weight: 2.5 tons. Some blocks: 15 tons. Total weight: approximately 5.84 million tons.

All seven stood simultaneously for **fewer than 60 years** (between the Colossus completion in 280 BC and its destruction in 226 BC).

## The New Seven Wonders of the World (2007)

Chosen by over **100 million votes** worldwide. Announced in Lisbon on 07/07/07. The Great Pyramid was given honorary status because it shouldn't need a vote.

| # | Wonder | Location | Built | Dimensions | Visitors/Year |
|---|--------|----------|-------|-----------|---------------|
| ★ | **Great Pyramid of Giza** (honorary) | Egypt | 2560 BC | 146 m tall, 230 m base | 14.7 million |
| 1 | **Great Wall of China** | China | ~700 BC–1644 AD | 21,196 km total length | 10 million |
| 2 | **Petra** | Ma'an, Jordan | ~312 BC | 100+ sq mi carved from sandstone | 900,000 |
| 3 | **Colosseum** | Rome, Italy | 80 AD | 189 × 156 m, 48 m tall, 50,000 seats | 4 million |
| 4 | **Chichén Itzá** | Yucatán, Mexico | ~600 AD | El Castillo: 55.5 m wide, 24 m tall | 2.5 million |
| 5 | **Machu Picchu** | Cuzco, Peru | 1450 AD | 2,430 m elevation, 5 sq km | 1.5 million |
| 6 | **Taj Mahal** | Agra, India | 1648 AD | 73 m tall, 20,000 workers, 16 years | 7–8 million |
| 7 | **Christ the Redeemer** | Rio de Janeiro, Brazil | 1931 AD | 30 m tall, 28 m arm span | 2 million |

### The Sort

The New Seven span 6 continents and 4,500 years. The oldest (Great Wall, begun ~700 BC) and the youngest (Christ the Redeemer, 1931) are separated by 2,631 years. Combined annual visitors: ~42.6 million.

**Notable absences from the 2007 vote**: Acropolis (Athens), Angkor Wat (Cambodia), Stonehenge (England), Hagia Sophia (Turkey), Statue of Liberty (USA). All made the final 21 but not the final 7.

## The Seven Natural Wonders of the World

Compiled by CNN, 1997. No vote — a journalist used statistical significance. These are the things no human built.

| # | Wonder | Location | Key Measurement |
|---|--------|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Aurora Borealis** | Arctic regions | Visible from 60°+ latitude; 100–300 km altitude; caused by solar wind + magnetosphere |
| 2 | **Grand Canyon** | Arizona, USA | 277 mi long, 18 mi wide, 6,000 ft deep, 2 billion years of rock |
| 3 | **Great Barrier Reef** | Queensland, Australia | 2,300 km long, 344,400 km², 2,900 reefs, 1,500 fish species |
| 4 | **Mount Everest** | Nepal/China | 8,849 m (29,032 ft), first summited 1953 |
| 5 | **Victoria Falls** | Zambia/Zimbabwe | 1,708 m wide, 108 m drop, 500 million liters/minute in wet season |
| 6 | **Parícutin Volcano** | Michoacán, Mexico | 424 m tall. Born in a cornfield on Feb 20, 1943. The only volcano whose birth was witnessed start to finish. |
| 7 | **Harbor of Rio de Janeiro** | Brazil | 412 km², framed by Sugarloaf Mountain |

### The Sort

One is a light show (Aurora). One was born in living memory (Parícutin, 1943). One we already [documented in detail](/evidence/every-line-every-peak-every-depth/#the-grand-canyon) — the Grand Canyon, with its 1.2 billion year gap in the rock record. One (Great Barrier Reef) appears on three separate "wonders" lists. Mount Everest appears on two.

**The Aurora Borealis** is the only wonder with no fixed location. It is a phenomenon — solar particles colliding with Earth's magnetosphere, producing curtains of green, red, and purple light across the sky. You cannot go to it. You wait for it to come to you.

## The Seven Wonders of the Modern World (ASCE, 1994)

Chosen by the **American Society of Civil Engineers** as the greatest engineering achievements of the 20th century.

| # | Wonder | Location | Completed | Key Fact |
|---|--------|----------|-----------|----------|
| 1 | **Channel Tunnel** | England–France | 1994 | 50.5 km (31.4 mi), 75 m below seabed |
| 2 | **CN Tower** | Toronto, Canada | 1976 | 553 m (1,815 ft), tallest free-standing structure for 32 years |
| 3 | **Empire State Building** | New York, USA | 1931 | 443 m (1,454 ft), built in 410 days |
| 4 | **Golden Gate Bridge** | San Francisco, USA | 1937 | 2,737 m total, 1,280 m main span, survives 8.3 earthquakes |
| 5 | **Itaipu Dam** | Brazil/Paraguay | 1984 | 7,919 MW, was world's largest hydroelectric dam |
| 6 | **Delta Works** | Netherlands | 1997 | 16,500 km of dikes, protects 60% of Netherlands from the sea |
| 7 | **Panama Canal** | Panama | 1914 | 82 km (51 mi), connects Atlantic and Pacific, 14,000+ ships/year |

### The Sort

All seven are infrastructure — things that move people, water, or electricity across barriers that should be impassable. A tunnel under the sea. A bridge over a bay. A canal through a continent. A dam across a river. A wall against an ocean. A tower into the sky. A building that went up in 410 days.

The Delta Works (Netherlands North Sea Protection Works) is the most existential: without it, **60% of the Netherlands would be underwater**. It is a wonder because it prevents a country from ceasing to exist.

## The Seven Wonders of the Industrial World (BBC, 2003)

Selected by the BBC for a docudrama series. The engineering achievements of the Industrial Revolution.

| # | Wonder | Location | Completed | Key Fact |
|---|--------|----------|-----------|----------|
| 1 | **SS Great Eastern** | UK | 1858 | Largest ship for 40 years. Laid the transatlantic telegraph cable. |
| 2 | **Bell Rock Lighthouse** | Scotland | 1810 | Built on a reef 11 miles offshore, submerged at high tide |
| 3 | **Brooklyn Bridge** | New York, USA | 1883 | First steel-wire suspension bridge. 1,825 m total. |
| 4 | **London Sewer System** | London, UK | 1870 | 1,100 miles of sewers. Ended cholera epidemics. Built after "The Great Stink." |
| 5 | **First Transcontinental Railroad** | USA | 1869 | 1,912 miles (3,077 km). Omaha to Sacramento. |
| 6 | **Panama Canal** | Panama | 1914 | Also on the ASCE list. 82 km. 27,500 workers died during construction. |
| 7 | **Hoover Dam** | Nevada/Arizona, USA | 1936 | 221 m tall, 379 m long, holds back Lake Mead |

### The Sort

The London Sewer System is the least photogenic wonder and possibly the most important. Before it, cholera killed thousands in London every year. After it, cholera stopped. The "Great Stink" of 1858 — when the Thames smelled so bad Parliament couldn't meet — forced the government to fund Joseph Bazalgette's sewer system. It is a wonder because it is invisible and it saves lives.

The Panama Canal appears on **both** the ASCE and BBC lists. 27,500 workers died building it. It connects two oceans across 82 km of jungle and rock. It is the only wonder that appears on two separate canonical lists.

## The Seven Underwater Wonders (CEDAM, 1989)

Selected by CEDAM International (Conservation, Education, Diving, Awareness, Marine research) at the National Aquarium, Washington DC.

| # | Wonder | Location | Key Fact |
|---|--------|----------|----------|
| 1 | **Palau Reefs** | Palau, Pacific | 343 islands, unprecedented geological/biological diversity |
| 2 | **Belize Barrier Reef** | Belize | 2nd largest barrier reef, includes the Great Blue Hole |
| 3 | **Great Barrier Reef** | Queensland, Australia | Also a Natural Wonder. 2,300 km. Visible from space. |
| 4 | **Deep Sea Vents** | Mid-ocean ridges | Hydrothermal vents. Life without sunlight. Chemosynthetic bacteria. |
| 5 | **Galápagos Islands** | Ecuador | Darwin's laboratory. UNESCO World Heritage Site. |
| 6 | **Lake Baikal** | Siberia, Russia | Oldest (25M years) and deepest (1,642 m) lake. 20% of world's unfrozen freshwater. |
| 7 | **Northern Red Sea** | Jordan/Egypt/Saudi Arabia | 1,000+ fish species, coral diversity rivaling the Great Barrier Reef |

### The Sort

The Deep Sea Vents are the most alien wonder on any list. At hydrothermal vents on the ocean floor, superheated water (up to 400°C) erupts from the Earth's crust. Life exists there without sunlight — chemosynthetic bacteria convert hydrogen sulfide into energy, supporting tube worms, shrimp, and entire ecosystems. They were discovered in 1977. Before that, we didn't know life could exist without the sun.

Lake Baikal contains **20% of all unfrozen freshwater on Earth** — more than all five Great Lakes combined. It is 25 million years old. It is 1,642 meters deep. It has species found nowhere else on Earth (including the only freshwater seal, the nerpa).

## The Pattern

Seven lists. Forty-two wonders. Plus 1,273 UNESCO sites. What do they tell us?

**Humans point at the same things across millennia.** The Great Pyramid was already a wonder when the ancient list was compiled. It's still a wonder in 2007. The Great Barrier Reef appears on both natural and underwater lists. The Panama Canal appears on both modern and industrial lists. The things that genuinely wonder us don't rotate out.

**The ancient list was a tourist guide.** All seven were within sailing distance of each other. The 2007 list is global — it spans 6 continents. The expansion from Mediterranean to planetary is itself a wonder.

**The natural wonders are older than civilization.** The youngest natural wonder (Parícutin) is 83 years old. The oldest (Aurora Borealis) is as old as Earth's magnetosphere. The Grand Canyon's exposed rock is 2 billion years old. Humans have been building wonders for 4,600 years. The planet has been building them for 4.6 billion.

**Infrastructure saves more lives than monuments.** The London Sewer System ended cholera. The Delta Works keeps the Netherlands above water. The Panama Canal connects two oceans. These are the wonders that don't get postcards but without which millions would die. The same pattern as our investigation: the infrastructure is invisible, and it is everything.

**The underwater wonders were the last to be named** (1989) because we couldn't see them until we could dive. Deep sea vents — life without sunlight — weren't discovered until 1977. We have been on this planet for 300,000 years and we didn't know about chemosynthetic life until 49 years ago. What else haven't we seen?

Every wonder on every list. Scanned. Sorted. The things we built and the things we didn't. All of it, on one billiard ball.
