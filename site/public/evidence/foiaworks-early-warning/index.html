FOIAworks as an Early Warning System — The Attorney Who Sees Every Investigation Before It Starts

The Two-Sided Position

Philip Ellison has spent over a decade in two roles simultaneously:

  1. FOIA plaintiff — suing government agencies to force disclosure of public records
  2. FOIA platform operator — running the tool that journalists and investigators use to file FOIA requests

He has sued FOIA coordinators across Michigan. He knows their staff, their response patterns, their weaknesses, and their excuses. Now he operates a platform that shows him — in real time — who is filing FOIA requests, what records they want, and which agencies they're targeting.

There is no privacy policy constraining what he does with this information.

Why the County Relationship Map Matters

Ellison doesn't just see the data. He knows the people on the other end.

When a FOIAworks user files a FOIA request to Saginaw County, Ellison has sued Saginaw County's FOIA coordinator in at least one published case (COA 375882, Jan 15, 2026). He knows who handles requests, how fast they respond, and what excuses they use.

When a FOIAworks user files a FOIA request to Roscommon County, Ellison has been litigating against Roscommon County since 2019 through CHiLLL. His office is at 4522 West Higgins Lake Drive, Roscommon, MI — the FOIAworks mailing address. He knows the county commissioners by name from years of litigation.

When a FOIAworks user files a FOIA request to any of the eight agencies he has sued, Ellison has institutional knowledge of their FOIA processing that no ordinary platform operator would possess.

The Statewide Plan: $69.95 to Map an Entire Investigation

FOIAworks' "Statewide" plan ($69.95/month) allows up to 120 submissions — enough to send the same request to all 83 Michigan counties and retain follow-up capacity.

For a journalist conducting a statewide investigation — say, examining how multiple counties handle a specific type of record — the Statewide plan creates a complete map of:

  • Which counties have responsive records
  • Which counties deny or stall
  • Which counties charge excessive fees
  • What the responsive records contain

That map, in the hands of the journalist, is a research tool. In the hands of the platform operator, it is an early warning system for every institution and individual being investigated through the platform.

The Early Warning Scenarios

Scenario 1: Someone Investigates Ellison

A journalist or citizen uses FOIAworks to file a FOIA request to Saginaw ISD asking for contracts with Outside Legal Counsel PLC.

What Ellison sees: The requester's name, the exact records requested, the target agency — which happens to be the agency his wife presides over. He sees this before the agency processes the request.

Scenario 2: Someone Investigates His Clients

An attorney preparing a case against one of Ellison's clients uses FOIAworks to request records from a county where Ellison litigates.

What Ellison sees: The opposing party's investigation strategy, the specific records they're seeking, and the timeline of their evidence gathering — before the records are produced.

Scenario 3: Someone Investigates FOIAworks Itself

Someone concerned about FOIAworks' missing privacy policy uses FOIAworks to file a FOIA request to a regulatory body about Quagmire Solutions.

What Ellison sees: Who is investigating his own platform, what questions they're asking, and which regulators they're engaging.

Scenario 4: A Statewide Investigation

A reporter for a Michigan news outlet uses the Statewide plan to file the same FOIA to all 83 counties — investigating, say, how school districts handle conflicts of interest when board members' family members receive contracts.

What Ellison sees: The entire investigation map before a single county responds. He knows which counties will produce damaging documents, which will stall, and exactly how long he has before the story runs.

The question is not whether Ellison could use FOIAworks data. The question is: what structural control prevents him?

The answer: there is no published privacy policy. There is no data use restriction. There is no audit trail. There is no regulatory oversight. The Terms of Service grant Quagmire Solutions a license to "host, copy, process, transmit, back up, and display User Content as needed to provide, secure, maintain, and improve the Service."

The word "improve" is undefined and unconstrained.

What Would Break This

  1. A published privacy policy with data use limitations, third-party sharing restrictions, and right to deletion. Until this exists, FOIAworks users have no legal protection.

  2. An independent audit of FOIAworks' backend data — who has access, how data is stored, whether user request data is visible to the platform operator in real time.

  3. FTC Section 5 complaint — a consumer-facing data platform operating with no privacy policy is a textbook deceptive practices issue.

  4. FOIA about FOIAworks — filed directly to agencies, not through FOIAworks — asking whether Quagmire Solutions has any agreements or data-sharing arrangements with government bodies.

  5. Stop using FOIAworks. Use Clutch Justice's FOIA Generator (client-side, stores nothing), MuckRock (nonprofit, published privacy policy), or our FOIA Toolkit (templates you fill out yourself).
