The UPEPA Contradiction

Michigan's Uniform Public Expression Protection Act (UPEPA, 2025 PA 52) is the state's anti-SLAPP statute. It provides expedited relief and mandatory fee-shifting when a lawsuit targets protected expression.

Ellison's contradictory positions on UPEPA:

CasePosition
Ellison v. Consumers Energy (26-000243-CZ)Filed UPEPA motion March 26, 2026. Argued UPEPA applies retroactively to causes of action asserted after March 24, 2026, even in cases filed before the effective date.
Ellison v. Williams (25-2441-CZ)Opposes defendant's UPEPA motion. Argues the statute does not apply.

He invoked anti-SLAPP to fight a utility company while running a SLAPP suit against a journalist. The judicial estoppel argument is straightforward: the same attorney cannot take mutually exclusive legal positions in the same court, in overlapping timeframes.

The Void Lawsuit

The SLAPP suit (25-2441-CZ) was filed in violation of the federal bankruptcy automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362(a). Under Kalb v. Feuerstein, 308 U.S. 433 (1940), proceedings conducted in violation of the automatic stay are void ab initio. Ellison never sought relief from the stay in the Western District.

Per Clutch Justice, May 31, 2026: "Every act taken in the proceeding has been void from inception."

Nine months of litigation — including a coercive stipulated order obtained while the defendant was pro se, four motions to strike, a fabricated witness, a false representation about an AGC complaint, and a COA sanctions motion — were conducted in a case that was void from filing.
