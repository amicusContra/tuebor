### I. The Money

In 2026, the State of Michigan sends approximately **$9,608 per pupil** in foundation allowance to public school academies — the legal name for charter schools. This is taxpayer money. Your money. It comes from sales tax, income tax, property tax, lottery revenue, and federal pass-throughs.

For a charter school with 200 students, that's **$1.92 million per year**.

For a charter school with 400 students, that's **$3.84 million per year**.

The money is supposed to educate children. That's it. That's the entire purpose. Not to enrich operators. Not to pay management fees. Not to fund consulting firms. Not to line the pockets of convicted felons. To educate children.

Here is what happened instead.

---

### II. What the Money Was Supposed to Do

Michigan's charter school law — **Public Act 362 of 1993** — was drafted by the law firm Dykema Gossett at the request of Governor John Engler and championed by Betsy DeVos. The stated purpose was noble:

> Give parents choices. Create competition. Drive innovation. Raise standards. Close the achievement gap. Lift children out of poverty through education.

The law created a system where:
1. **State universities** authorize charter schools (Central Michigan University authorizes 60+)
2. **Management companies** run the schools for a fee
3. **Per-pupil funding** flows from the state to the school
4. **No cap** on the number of charters (cap removed in 2011 with DeVos lobbying through GLEP)
5. **No powerful local oversight body** (Detroit Education Commission killed in 2016 by charter lobby)

That's the architecture. Here's what it built.

### III. Where the Money Actually Went

#### The Sweeps Contract

At Purpose Charter Academy, **The Purpose Group LLC** — a management company controlled by a single person, Brian Roderick Banks — takes **72.67% of school revenue** as a management fee.

That's not a typo. Seventy-two point six seven percent.

For a school receiving $1.92 million in public funding, that means approximately **$1.39 million** goes to Banks' management company. For services rendered. Without competitive bidding. Without independent audit of the management company's books. Without FOIA access to how the money is spent inside the LLC.

This is called a **sweeps contract**. It sweeps the public money out of the public entity (the school) and into the private entity (the LLC). Once the money crosses that line, it's gone. The school board — the one with a judge, an assistant attorney general, and a city council member on it — approved this arrangement.

**Statewide, sweeps contracts move approximately $1.3 billion per year** from public charter schools into private management companies. That's $1.3 billion in taxpayer money that is:
- Not subject to FOIA
- Not subject to detailed public audit
- Not subject to competitive bidding requirements
- Not required to demonstrate educational outcomes
