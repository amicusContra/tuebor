The Narrative Cycle

This is not a conspiracy. It’s an operating system.

Step 1: Fund the narrative. Walton pays for Roland Martin’s “School Choice Is the Black Choice” tour. Ten cities. Partnered with The 74 — a news site that Walton also funds, founded by an advocate who sits on the board of the DeVos-chaired school choice lobby. The message: charter schools = Black empowerment. Choice = freedom.

Step 2: Fund the credential pipeline. Walton pays for Pahara Institute fellowships. Pahara makes Brian Banks — a man with 9 criminal convictions — into a “Pahara Fellow.” The fellowship is designed to “strengthen values-driven leaders.” Banks’s bio now reads like a redemption narrative: “Having personally navigated the very systems that continue to fail many Detroit youth.”

What it doesn’t say: he defrauded a credit union, violated campaign finance laws, and runs a school where 3% of students are proficient in math while 72.67% of revenue flows to his single-member LLC.

Step 3: Fund the lobby. Walton pays MAPSA $4.3M+ to fight charter regulation in Michigan. Michigan is already the most deregulated charter state in America. Public Act 277 (2011) lifted the charter cap entirely. 80% of Michigan charters are run by for-profit companies. The result: a $1B/year industry with the weakest accountability in the nation.

Step 4: Shield with “Jim Crow 2.0.” Roland Martin — now a trusted voice in Black media — frames any attack on Black institutions as “Jim Crow 2.0.” The framing is real and valid when applied to voter suppression, redistricting, and defunding. But it creates an immune response that also shields charter extraction from scrutiny. Criticize a Black-led charter school? You’re attacking Black agency. Question the money flow? You’re questioning Black entrepreneurship.

Step 5: Celebrate at the dinner. Roland Martin is honored at the Detroit NAACP Freedom Fund Dinner. 10,000 attendees. Alongside Alice Thompson — a Black charter network leader whose organization received a Skillman Foundation planning grant. The credential is now circular: the narrative legitimized the schools, the schools legitimized the leaders, the leaders are honored by civil rights organizations, and the honor legitimizes the narrative.

What Roland Martin’s Audience Doesn’t Know

Roland Martin has 30 million views per month. He is honored by the Detroit NAACP. He uses “Jim Crow 2.0” with sincerity — and the concept is real.

But his “School Choice Is the Black Choice” initiative was co-produced with The 74 and funded by the same foundation that funds:

  • The fellowship that credentialed a 9-time convicted felon to run schools for Detroit’s most vulnerable children
  • The lobby that fights every attempt to regulate Michigan charter schools
  • The rating system that evaluates those same charter schools

His audience — predominantly Black, politically engaged, civil-rights-conscious — has been told that charter schools are the frontier of Black educational sovereignty. Some charter schools genuinely are.

MacDowell Preparatory Academy is not one of them.

3% math proficiency. 2 of 14 staff properly credentialed. $4.9M/year in public funds. 72.67% to a single-member LLC. And the man running it was made a “Pahara Fellow” with Walmart money.
