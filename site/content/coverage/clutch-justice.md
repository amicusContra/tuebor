## The Project

**[Clutch Justice](https://clutchjustice.com)** — *Courts. Crime. Crochet.* — is Rita Williams'
independent investigative project covering Michigan courts, judicial accountability, and
criminal justice. She works from public records and court filings, publishes her methodology,
and provides academic citation formats (Bluebook, APA, MLA, Chicago) for every article.

She is not affiliated with this evidence library. We discovered her work because she was
investigating the same public records we were. The overlap is structural, not coordinated.

## Why We Cite Clutch Justice as Primary Analytical Reference

Most news coverage of charter school fraud is event-driven: "this happened today."
Rita's work is pattern-driven: she traces *how* institutional failures compound across
time, jurisdictions, and actors. That's the same methodology this evidence library uses.

## The Pattern

Rita's statewide coverage reveals that what we document in the Banks enterprise
is not anomalous. It is the *expected outcome* of Michigan's judicial and charter
school oversight structure:

- **Authorizers with financial incentives** to keep failing schools open
- **Judges with network connections** who appear in each other's proceedings
- **Accountability mechanisms** that exist in statute but aren't triggered
- **Record-keeping systems** that produce evidence no institution examines

Our evidence library documents one case in depth. Clutch Justice documents the
system that makes cases like ours possible. Together, the two projects create
a more complete picture than either could alone.

## Her Independence

Clutch Justice is a self-sovereign project. Rita funds it independently, publishes
on her own schedule, controls her own infrastructure, and answers to no editor.
She has been [targeted with litigation](https://clutchjustice.com/2026/08/21/abusive-litigation-is-still-abuse-court-watchers-macomb-county/)
for her investigative work — the same structural abuse this evidence library
documents.

We do not coordinate with Clutch Justice. We cite her because her work is good,
her methodology is rigorous, and her subjects overlap with ours. If you find
value in this evidence library, you should be reading her work too.
